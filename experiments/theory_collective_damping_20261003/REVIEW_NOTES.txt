Theory review notes — 2026-10-03

1. The finite Schur update uses fixed rho, gains, trim, row normalization,
   retained physical ports and evaluation frequency. Both old/new hidden
   blocks must be nonsingular. It is not continuation of a moving root.
2. Hermitian indefiniteness requires linearly independent complex port
   paths and a nonzero update. Distinct bus labels do not imply that
   condition. Multiple delayed channels need not have the same sign pattern.
3. A low-rank incremental change does not imply low-rank total damping.
4. The PLL series is for the closed phase-tracking transfer of the actual
   detector-error delay and existing first-order frequency filter. Singular
   later reductions can change its order. No full s=0 grid reduction was run.
5. The graph-moment theorem permits nonsymmetric D0 through separate bL/bR,
   but assumes a symmetric connected L. Its nonnegative correction and
   minimum-flow interpretation additionally require symmetric D0.
6. A negative response moment forces overshoot for a stable integrable
   response. It does not give a numerical nadir, prove instability, or
   prove violation of the frozen 0.5 Hz limit.
7. The inertia coefficient in the response moment is not stored energy;
   physical SG inertia remains positive in the stable two-node example.
8. Mechanical torque-step COI RoCoF is not the campaign's windowed
   bus-voltage-phase RoCoF. The formal knapsack bound is not a certificate
   for the frozen IEEE-39 event problem.
9. The causal pre-delay theorem allows immediate current/DC/governor
   response. Only dependence on the specified PLL gain change is excluded.
10. No bibliography-first novelty claim is established. Rank-one update,
    Hermitian eigenvalue formulas, Green functions and transfer moments
    use standard mathematics. The physical claim requires comparison.
