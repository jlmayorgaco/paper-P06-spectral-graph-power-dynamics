# SAFE ALONE, UNSAFE TOGETHER

## Subtitle

Predicting Minimal Dynamic Incompatibility in SG-to-GFL Replacement Portfolios

## Panel 1 — The surprise

Four replacements. Fifteen proper portfolios are stable. The complete H4
portfolio is unstable at P4.

Large text: **15 SAFE / 1 UNSAFE**

## Panel 2 — Predict before full simulation

One all-SG network kernel K(s) plus four local Delta-Y substitutions predicts
the H4 failure before full-order reveal. V4: 16/16 verdicts, false-safe 0,
exact H and kappa=4.

The V9 census is shown as a negative scope test: 395/512, false-safe 116,
exact H recovery false.

## Panel 3 — Why

Show det(I+Q_H)=0 and the contextual return
R_i|R=Q_iR(I+Q_RR)^(-1)Q_Ri. Local factors remain regular while the collective
closure becomes singular. Label this classical block algebra applied to the
TX4 decomposition, not a new theorem.

## Panel 4 — Same penetration, different compatibility

Use only the near-match: six replacements, 0.2513% normalized MW/MVA mismatch;
one V9 portfolio unstable and one stable. State explicitly that the match is
not exact because no exact MW/MVA pair was available.

## Panel 5 — Fix

Keep grid, buses, and dispatch fixed. Change only g:
g=0.03625 gives alpha=+0.1270 s^-1; g=0.25 gives alpha=-0.01738 s^-1.

## Panel 6 — Nonlinear validation

Common +2% active-load pulse at bus 20 for 0.2 s: proper subset decays, H4
original grows, retuned H4 decays. This is nonlinear phasor-domain TDS, not
EMT. Add a small ANDES badge reading “limited independent phasor support;
custom GFL boundary not tested”.

## Bottom takeaway

Replacement safety is a portfolio property. Minimal incompatibility tells us
what fails. Contextual return explains why. Boundary retuning shows how to move
it. The claim is model-specific and the V9 limitation remains visible.
