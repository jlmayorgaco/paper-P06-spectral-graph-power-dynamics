GENERAL THEORY: NO IEEE39 OR 100-MW CONSTANT IN THE DEFINING EQUATIONS
User clarification: the network, disturbance and numerical limits below are only
an experimental instance. The theory takes them as inputs, not universal constants.
Let a network/model M, an operating-point set O, uncertainty set Xi, disturbance
family D and measurement operators H_f,H_r be declared. D must be bounded or
otherwise physically restricted; no finite grid can satisfy arbitrary disturbances.
For every operating point o, uncertainty xi and disturbance d(t;eta) in those sets,
  xdot=f(x,v,p,d;M,o,xi), 0=g(x,v,p,d;M,o,xi).
The index-one formulation requires g_v nonsingular on the considered trajectory.
The general derivative is v_p=-g_v^{-1}g_p, v_x=-g_v^{-1}g_x. It applies to
nonlinear algebraic load laws as well as the affine-Z special case implemented.
The closed finite rank-two update is restricted to that affine-voltage special
case; it is not asserted for an arbitrary nonlinear g.
With a nonsingular differential mass matrix M_d, first reduce F=M_d^{-1}f, and
include the derivatives of M_d in F_x,F_p. With a residual DAE R(z,zdot,p)=0,
the equivalent tangent equation is R_zdot*Sdot+R_z*S+R_p=0.

Define each security functional through a supremum, for example
  C_f(p)=sup_{o in O,xi in Xi,d in D,b in B,t in [0,H]}
         (|H_f[phi_b](t)|/fmax_b-1),
and likewise C_r,C_V,C_I,C_E plus the declared terminal/stability condition.
The general co-design is min J(p) subject to every C_l(p)<=0 and device bounds.
Measurement windows, nominal frequency, powers, horizon and hardware limits are
parameters. The differentiable measurement operators act on phase sensitivities.

The robust search contains two coupled optimization problems:
  design: improve p using the currently active disturbances and time/location cuts;
  separation: maximize a normalized violation over D,O,Xi and time/location.
A newly found violation becomes a cut/scenario and the design is repaired.
Adjoints can also differentiate the response with respect to disturbance amplitude,
location-dependent injection, duration and smooth uncertainty parameters. Discrete
fault locations/topologies require enumeration or a justified discrete search;
fault times/reset maps require event-time and saltation terms.
Six endpoint simulations do not solve this general separation problem. A global
robust-feasibility statement requires a global separation bound or another valid
certificate; a local adversarial search supplies counterexamples, not such a bound.
The current executable is the finite-D specialization. Continuous adversarial
separation, uncertainty sweeps and terminal certificates are extensions to validate.
For tied worst cases, retain their gradients as a bundle (Danskin-type envelope
derivatives under the usual compactness/smoothness assumptions). Do not assume a
single differentiable maximum. No monotonicity in disturbance size is assumed.

SCIENTIFIC CONTRACT
Problem: maximize the power-weighted fraction of synchronous generation replaced
by co-located grid-following converters under declared disturbances and limits.
Gap to test: equilibrium damping and isolated PLL tuning can admit designs whose
nonlinear trajectories violate frequency/RoCoF limits. This is supported by the
previous IEEE39 bus-8 counterexample, not a universal statement about every grid.
Proposed contribution: derive a network-coupled marginal value of SG replacement
from the complete nonlinear DAE, and use it to co-design placement, fractions and
PLL gains along an explicitly validated replacement frontier.
Falsification: compare against uniform replacement, modal-only co-design and
nonlinear co-design without modal guidance, on identical models and constraints.
Consequence: identify how many MW can be converted, where synchronous support
must remain, and which disturbance, location and physical limit set the boundary.

MODEL AND SCOPE
p=(rho, kappa_p, kappa_i), Kp=Kp_ref*exp(kappa_p), Ki=Ki_ref*exp(kappa_i).
Reference gains carry the units. The executable uses numerical log gains in fixed
declared units, which differs only by a constant offset of these coordinates.
J(p)=sum_i P0_i (1-rho_i). Minimize J; replaced fraction=1-J/sum_i P0_i.
This is a dispatch-weighted replacement fraction of co-located aggregates. It is
not the fraction of units retired, installed renewable capacity, or delivered energy.
Internal SG equations retain their original per-unit inertia and parameters;
terminal current is scaled by 1-rho. Scaling inertia again double-counts removal.
PLL gains do not provide primary energy reserves or synthetic inertia by themselves.
The implemented GFL includes PLL, current filter/controller and DC-link controller.

For the frozen constant-impedance-load model, each SG stator map at fixed internal
state is exactly affine in the terminal voltage: i_s=D_i(x_i)v_i+c_i(x_i).
G(x,rho,d)=Y_Z(d)+sum_i (1-rho_i) S_i D_i(x_i) S_i^T.
h(x,rho)=sum_i S_i[(1-rho_i)c_i(x_i)+rho_i j_g(x_i)].
g(x,v,p,d)=Gv+h=0. If G is nonsingular, v=-G^{-1}h exactly.
The nonlinear vector field is F(x,p,d)=f(x,v(x,p,d),p,d).
No Taylor truncation of the plant is introduced by this algebraic elimination.
Passive-bus Kron reduction further reduces the algebraic solve from 78 to 20 real
port coordinates, while retaining every SG/GFL dynamic state. It assumes the
passive block remains invertible; changed loads/topologies require rebuilding it.
General constant-power load models require a nonlinear algebraic solve instead.

CONDITIONAL STATE-INDEPENDENT ALGEBRAIC REGULARITY
For a port-reduced real admittance K, define H=-(K+K^T)/2. If H>=gamma I,
gamma>0, and each SG stator map D_i is skew-symmetric, then
  G_port=K+sum_i (1-rho_i) S_i D_i S_i^T,
  -sym(G_port)=H, sigma_min(G_port)>=gamma, ||G_port^{-1}||_2<=1/gamma.
Proof: gamma||z||^2 <= -z^T G_port z <= ||z|| ||G_port z||.
In this particular machine dataset, Rs=0 and Xd''=Xq''=X'', giving
  D_i=(S_i_rating/S_base)/(omega_i X_i'') * J, J=[0,-1;1,0].
Thus the bound holds independently of angles, replacement fractions and internal
electromagnetic states, conditional on nonzero machine speed and fixed admittance.
Together with an invertible passive block, it proves algebraic solvability of the
full network. It does not prove dynamic stability, voltage limits, or a safe
all-GFL operating point. More general machines/loads need another condition.
The nominal and six declared Z-load cases have positive numerical gamma values
(minimum approximately 0.5133; see algebraic_regularity.csv). The eigenvalue
calculation is floating-point, not a formal interval certificate; this finite
case audit does not certify a continuous disturbance family.
For a distributed or approximate adjoint solve G^T zeta=b with residual r,
  ||zeta_hat-zeta|| <= ||r||/gamma.
The corresponding local replacement-score error is at most
  ||i_g-i_s|| * ||r||/gamma
at that instant, before integration and other sensitivity errors. Local device
data supplies the current mismatch; a network solve or converged distributed
iteration supplies the global influence. Purely local measurements alone do not
identify the robust network-wide replacement capacity.

EXACT MOVING REFERENCE FRAME FOR FAST NONLINEAR ACCEPTANCE
The equations are equivariant under a common phase rotation. Let T_alpha denote
that group action and q(y)=d T_alpha(y)/d alpha at alpha=0. Choose a phase coordinate
y_r with q_r=1 and keep y_r fixed. Represent x=T_alpha(y), with
  alpha_dot=Omega(y)=F_r(y),
  ydot=F(y)-Omega(y)*q(y).
The redundant fixed y_r is replaced in the state vector by alpha, so no physical
mode or common frequency is discarded. The exact Jacobian on the unfixed
coordinates is F_y-q*(dOmega/dy)-Omega*Dq, with the alpha column zero and the
alpha row dOmega/dy. Reconstruct voltages as v=T_alpha(v_relative).
Thus terminal phase is alpha+phi_relative. Frequency/RoCoF must include both
terms; omitting alpha would erase the physical common-frequency response.
This avoids resolving a continuing common rotation in every converter current
after a load step. A benchmark compares the exact coordinate change against the
original coordinates; see rotating_frame_audit.toml. It is a numerical acceleration,
not an equilibrium linearization, dynamic truncation or stability certificate.

EXACT LOCAL-TO-NETWORK REPLACEMENT DERIVATIVE
At fixed internal states in a fixed support:
  v_{rho_i} = -G^{-1} S_i (j_g,i-i_s,i).
The mismatch is local, but propagation through G^{-1} is network dependent.
Thus a local damping score alone cannot determine the network replacement value.
For a finite delta in rho_i at fixed states, with Z_ii=S_i^T G^{-1} S_i:
  v_new = v-delta G^{-1}S_i[I_2-delta D_i Z_ii]^{-1}(j_g,i-i_s,i).
  det(G_new)/det(G)=det(I_2-delta D_i Z_ii).
This rank-two identity predicts algebraic response and regularity at that instant;
it is not a formula for admissible dynamic replacement after a disturbance.
At the matched pre-event equilibrium the mismatch vanishes. This does not imply
zero trajectory sensitivity: the mismatch develops during the transient.

NONLINEAR TRAJECTORY SENSITIVITIES
At every point of the actual nonlinear trajectory:
  v_x=-g_v^{-1}g_x; v_p=-g_v^{-1}g_p.
  F_x=f_x+f_v v_x; F_p=f_p+f_v v_p.
  Sdot=F_x S+F_p, S(0)=dx0/dp.
Differentiating a nonlinear simulation is not replacing it by one equilibrium
linearization. The matrices here vary with the nonlinear trajectory.
For our common frozen trim, x0 is independent of interior rho and PLL gains.
For other dispatches, consistent equilibrium differentiation is mandatory.
The installed PLL uses e_i=-sin(theta_i)u_r+cos(theta_i)u_i and
  theta_dot=omega; tau omega_dot=xi+Kp e-omega; xi_dot=Ki e.
Its direct derivatives are F_{log Kp}=Kp e/tau in the omega equation and
F_{log Ki}=Ki e in the xi equation. Network feedback is retained in F_x.

AN ANALYTICAL PLL INITIALIZATION, WITH ITS LIMITATION
At a fixed stiff terminal voltage V>0, the isolated installed PLL has polynomial
  tau*s^3+s^2+Kp*V*s+Ki*V.
Routh-Hurwitz gives Kp>tau*Ki in addition to positive gains. Matching it to
  tau*(s+a)*(s^2+2*zeta*omega_n*s+omega_n^2)
gives a=1/tau-2*zeta*omega_n>0,
  Kp=tau*(omega_n^2+2*a*zeta*omega_n)/V,
  Ki=tau*a*omega_n^2/V.
These formulas can initialize gain choices with specified isolated-loop poles.
They do not include converter current/DC-loop or network interactions, and do
not define optimal gains for a multi-converter network. This local calculation
is a useful baseline precisely because the full-network method can falsify it.

ADJOINT INTERPRETATION (DERIVED; PROTOTYPE IMPLEMENTS FORWARD TANGENTS)
For a scalar active path constraint, integrate the reduced adjoint backwards:
  -lambda_dot=F_x^T lambda, between observation times, with output-gradient jumps.
Let zeta=G^{-T} f_v^T lambda. Then the trajectory contribution to its gradient is
  d c / d rho_i = - integral zeta_i^T (j_g,i-i_s,i) dt
plus explicit voltage-output terms at the observation times and any initial-state
terms. Gain gradients are integral lambda_omega Kp e/tau and lambda_xi Ki e.
This is the useful analytical result: a network adjoint weights a local current
mismatch. It assigns replacement value with respect to the actual active limit.
It does not require claiming that all nodes or graph modes are interchangeable.
The transpose convention uses real rectangular voltage coordinates.
For clarity, a windowed RoCoF constraint at a fixed active (bus,time) has phase
weights a_m=sign(r_T)*[1,-2,1]_m/(2*pi*T^2*rmax), m=0,1,2, at t_m=t*-m*T.
Writing H(x,p)=unwrapped angle of that bus, its exact smooth-regime derivative is
  dc/dp = lambda(0)^T*x0_p + integral lambda^T*F_p dt + sum_m a_m*H_p(t_m),
  lambda(t_m^-)=lambda(t_m^+)+a_m*H_x(t_m)^T, lambda(t>t*)=0.
The prescribed pre-event history contributes its own derivative when it depends
on the design. Frequency uses [1,-1] instead. This explicitly includes voltage
feedthrough and prevents accidentally discarding the algebraic phase jump.

MEASUREMENTS AND PEAK CONSTRAINTS
Use the unwrapped terminal-voltage phase phi, not the PLL's own estimated frequency.
For an explicitly declared causal window T:
  f_T(t)=[phi(t)-phi(t-T)]/(2*pi*T).
  r_T(t)=[phi(t)-2phi(t-T)+phi(t-2T)]/(2*pi*T^2).
At a voltage/load discontinuity include the post-event algebraic phase jump.
Negative-time history is the equilibrium history. At window discontinuities,
check both one-sided values when defining a continuous-time supremum.
Voltage-phase derivative: phi_p=(u_r u_i,p-u_i u_r,p)/|v|^2.
Apply the same finite-window operators to phi_p to get frequency/RoCoF gradients.
For a unique active sample c=|f_T|-fmax, dc=sign(f_T) df_T. At tied peaks use
multiple active cuts/a gradient bundle; differentiating one arbitrary peak can fail.
The same applies to voltage, current, DC energy, and multiple disturbances.
Grid sampling alone is not a continuous-time certificate; refine peaks, solver
tolerances and windows, or bound the interpolation remainder.

ITERATIVE METHOD
1. Establish an independently validated feasible anchor and a fixed active support.
2. Simulate the nonlinear DAE; find the largest normalized constraints and their
   locations/times. Enrich the active set when new peaks/disturbances are found.
3. Compute exact nonlinear sensitivities (or adjoints). Include gain bounds.
4. Form a trust-region quadratic subproblem:
   min_delta grad J^T delta + 0.5 delta^T H delta + penalty*sum(slack)
   s.t. c_l + grad c_l^T delta <= slack_l, slack_l>=0, box bounds, ||D delta||<=Delta.
   Slacks restore feasibility in subproblems; they do not waive final constraints.
5. Validate the trial with the original nonlinear model and reject incomplete
   simulations or violations. Shrink the radius if the predicted reduction fails.
   The extended prototype may first try a bounded minimum-norm feasibility
   correction using the same constraint Jacobian, while retaining objective
   improvement. Each correction is independently simulated; it is not accepted
   solely from its first-order prediction. This follows curved active boundaries
   but is not itself a proof of convergence to a KKT point.
6. Refine active times and the discretization as the boundary is approached.
   In the initial run a late bus-29 event made the 20 s gradient horizon inadequate;
   the code detected this and the resumed search uses the full 60 s gradients.
7. If a fraction reaches an endpoint, remove the physical device states, rebuild
   trim/gauge and validate that support as a separate branch. Also test restoring
   support where a full-removal branch fails. Do not retain ghost-state poles.
8. Stop only with declared feasibility tolerance and stationarity/KKT residual,
   or label the result best feasible found. Compare independent starts/supports.
This is constrained sequential optimization, not expectation-maximization:
there is no probabilistic latent variable or likelihood defining an EM E-step.

ANALYTICAL GAIN COMPENSATION AND NODE EXCHANGEABILITY
For an active constraint set, partition its Jacobian as [A_rho A_kappa].
Given a replacement step d_rho, let b=-c_active-A_rho*d_rho. The minimum weighted
gain change satisfying A_kappa*d_kappa=b is, when feasible,
  d_kappa = W_kappa^{-1} A_kappa^T
            (A_kappa W_kappa^{-1} A_kappa^T)^dagger b.
W_kappa is positive definite. The equality must be checked: the pseudoinverse
does not make an inconsistent target feasible. Gain bounds require the bounded
QP instead. The nonlinear acceptance step is still mandatory.
If the joint QP contains a gain linear term ell_k or a cross Hessian block
H_kr*d_rho, first set a=-H_kk^{-1}(ell_k+H_kr*d_rho), then the conditional solution is
  d_kappa=a+H_kk^{-1}A_kappa^T
            (A_kappa H_kk^{-1}A_kappa^T)^dagger*(b-A_kappa*a).
This is the corresponding general formula for the joint modal-metric QP.
This provides a closed analytical control step conditional on a local replacement
step and active set, rather than pretending there is a universal closed-form optimum.

To exchange the same number of MW between nodes i and j, set
  d_rho = delta_MW*(e_i/P0_i-e_j/P0_j).
Without retuning, first-order interchangeability on the selected constraint vector
requires A_rho*d_rho=0. With unconstrained retuning, it requires
  (I-A_kappa A_kappa^dagger)*A_rho*d_rho=0.
With finite gain limits, solve the bounded compensation QP and report its remaining
constraint residual. Large compensation or a near-singular gain Jacobian means
that formal unconstrained interchangeability is not practically available.
Preserving the active constraint vector locally is weaker than preserving the
entire nonlinear response or stability region. Validate finite MW exchanges with
the nonlinear simulator; this is a directly falsifiable beyond-nodal experiment.
The implemented joint trust-region QP contains this local compensation problem;
the local exchangeability map and finite-exchange experiments are saved under
stationarity and exchange_checkpoint, with their actual scope and residuals.

A REGULARIZED GEOMETRY OF REPLACEMENT AND CONTROL AUTHORITY
If A_kappa has full row rank, its unbounded column-space test can become vacuous:
every small constraint shift can formally be cancelled, possibly by enormous
gain changes. Keep the required gain effort and physical bounds in the analysis.
For weights W_c>0,W_k>0 and lambda>0 define the local exchange cost
  d_ij^2=min_delta_k ||A_rho*delta_rho+A_kappa*delta_k||_{W_c}^2
                           +lambda*||delta_k||_{W_k}^2,
with gain bounds included when imposed. Without bounds, put
  H_k=A_kappa^T W_c A_kappa+lambda W_k,
  delta_k=-H_k^{-1}A_kappa^T W_c A_rho*delta_rho.
The minimum is delta_rho^T M delta_rho with the positive-semidefinite matrix
  M=A_rho^T[W_c-W_c A_kappa H_k^{-1} A_kappa^T W_c]A_rho.
For equal-MW exchange use delta_rho=delta_MW*(e_i/P0_i-e_j/P0_j).
This gives a declared, constraint-weighted local geometry of node exchange and
control authority. It depends on the operating point, disturbance set, active
limits and weights; it is not a universal graph distance or a global equivalence.
The Schur-complement/least-squares algebra itself is established mathematics.
The scientific question is whether this application explains and improves the
validated replacement frontier compared with nodal/modal-only baselines.
Without a tie-breaking control-effort objective, maximum replacement generally
does not determine unique PLL gains. If unique implementable gains are desired,
minimize control effort lexicographically after the replacement objective, rather
than silently changing the capacity objective with an arbitrary penalty.
Finite exchange experiments are stored in exchange_checkpoint: the local 31/32
compensation was tested in the full nonlinear trajectory at 0.1 and 1 MW; the
bounded compensation tested for 35/36 did not recover the declared decay margin.
That failed compensation is not a global proof that no nonlinear retuning exists.

WHY PLL TUNING CANNOT REMOVE EVERY REPLACEMENT LIMIT
For the installed controller, a synchronized relative equilibrium with common
angular offset Omega satisfies theta_dot=Omega, omega_PLL=Omega, xi_PLL=Omega,
e_q=0 (Ki>0), constant dq currents and constant DC voltage. Kp and Ki disappear
from those equilibrium equations. Hence, within a fixed synchronized equilibrium
branch and with fixed converter power/resource setpoints, PLL gains affect
transient behavior and stability but cannot change its steady-state frequency,
power balance or voltages. A binding steady-state reserve/frequency limit is
therefore not generally repairable by retuning the PLL alone.
This statement is controller-specific: a controller that changes active-power
references through frequency feedback adds gains to the equilibrium equations
and must include its energy/headroom constraints. It does not contradict that case.
A useful general design architecture separates necessary post-disturbance
equilibrium/resource feasibility from transient/PLL/network feasibility; both
are required before claiming maximum admissible replacement.

ROLE OF MODAL AND GRAPH ANALYSIS
Use the physical Jacobian's quotient by the one rotational symmetry. For a simple
pole lambda_j with left/right vectors w_j,v_j:
  d lambda_j/dp = w_j^* (dA/dp) v_j / (w_j^* v_j).
Modal residues C v_j w_j^* B/(w_j^*v_j), not damping ratios alone, reveal which
disturbances excite a mode and which measured nodes observe it.
Use scaled modal gradients/residues to construct a positive trust metric, initialize
directions and explain the result; do not let a modal proxy override a failed TDS.
Near repeated/ill-conditioned poles use a spectral subspace/Schur analysis rather
than unstable individual-eigenvector derivatives.
Graph Fourier coordinates describe spatial structure. U^T G U generally remains
dense and state dependent; electrical graph eigenvalues are not temporal Hz.
If every graph mode is retained the transform is a change of coordinates, not an
approximation. Any truncation requires a residual bound and full-model validation.
Distributed polynomial filters are an optional execution layer for a future power
controller with known reserves; they are not needed for static offline PLL tuning.

OPTIONAL FREQUENCY/ROCOF POWER SUPPORT: A DIFFERENT CONTROL LAYER
If the hardware has usable headroom and a DC energy source, augment each GFL with
  tau_f * fhat_dot = f_meas - fhat,
  tau_r * z_dot = fhat - z, rhat=(fhat-z)/tau_r,
  eta_dot=fhat,
  DeltaP_cmd=sat_{P,I,E}(-k_f*fhat-k_r*rhat-k_int*eta+u_graph),
where all frequencies are deviations in explicitly declared units. The derivative
filter is realizable; raw differentiation of noisy frequency is not assumed.
The saturation, anti-windup, DC-source and energy dynamics must be included in the
DAE. These k_f,k_r,k_int are distinct from the PLL's Kp,Ki. The same sensitivity
and constrained co-design framework applies after adding the new states/gains.
For an undirected communication Laplacian L, u_graph=sum_{l=0}^q a_l L^l fhat
can be evaluated by q neighbor exchanges. In graph coordinates its response is
h(lambda)=sum_l a_l lambda^l; communication delay/noise and a missing neighbor
change the realized dynamics and must be included in validation. This controller
is a proposed extension, not part of the numerical replacement percentages here.

OPTIMALITY AND A PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
Let mu_l be active-constraint multipliers. For an interior replacement fraction:
  P0_i = sum_l mu_l (d c_l / d rho_i).
Hence the constraint-weighted marginal security cost per converted MW equals one
at stationary interior nodes; bound-active nodes satisfy the corresponding inequalities.
For interior PLL gains: sum_l mu_l (d c_l / d kappa_i)=0.
This provides a testable explanation of why a node retains SG and whether further
gain tuning can buy additional replacement. Multipliers are meaningful only after
the constraints and KKT residual are validated and constraint qualification holds.
Local KKT stationarity is not global maximality. A global statement requires an
upper bound on attainable replacement (equivalently a lower bound on retained SG),
e.g. a valid reserve/energy relaxation or verified branch-and-bound. A conservative
Lyapunov/SOS feasible set gives a lower bound on admissible replacement, not an
upper bound on the true physical optimum. Do not invert those bounds.

A possible upper-bound route for the asymptotically convergent step-disturbance
problem is to drop the transient equations but retain necessary post-disturbance
power balance, voltage/resource limits and steady-frequency limits. Globally solve
or outer-relax that equilibrium-feasibility problem (with valid bilinear/AC-network
relaxations); its attainable replacement is an upper bound for that asymptotic
problem. A locally solved equilibrium optimization is not such an upper bound.
This extra equilibrium condition is not automatically necessary for an arbitrary
finite-horizon-only problem; the terminal convergence assumption matters.
The familiar aggregate-inertia RoCoF formula assumes coherent frequency and a
specific initial response/measurement. It is not a valid universal upper bound
for local finite-window voltage-phase RoCoF with algebraic jumps and PLL dynamics.

HYBRID AND IMPLEMENTATION LIMITS
Hard AVR/governor saturation changes the vector field. Smooth-mode sensitivities
cannot be propagated blindly through event crossings. Either include saltation/
event-time derivatives with transversality checks, or constrain the search to a
declared nonsaturated operating region. Grazing/chattering is not a smooth case.
The prototype audits derivatives inside that region and uses an actuator margin
when searching it. Full saturation-aware optimization is a separate extension.
The SDIRK2 trajectory derivative differentiates the implicit numerical stages,
including the implicit voltage solve. It is exact for the discretized equations
up to Newton tolerance; convergence to the continuous gradient needs refinement.
Final trajectory acceptance uses adaptive Rodas5P, independent of the SDIRK mesh.

NOVELTY POSITIONING (TARGET, NOT AN EXHAUSTIVE PRIORITY CLAIM)
Trajectory sensitivities for transient security are established: Tang/McCalley,
2012, https://certs.lbl.gov/publications/efficient-transient-stability.html .
End-to-end differentiable simulation including initialization is also established:
Bossart and Hodge, 2026, "Differentiable programming for transient power system
simulations", https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijepes.2026.111825 . Their Julia/PSID code:
https://github.com/m-bossart/DifferentiablePowerSystemSimulations . The general
gradient machinery is therefore explicitly prior art, not the novelty claim here.
In this replacement experiment the preserved trim makes x0_p=0 for rho/PLL gains;
other design variables need initialization sensitivities, as that work emphasizes.
Unified inverter-rich stability-constrained optimization already exists: Chu/Teng,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12151 .
DAE dissipativity and IEEE39 certificates already exist: Jensen et al.,
https://arxiv.org/html/2308.08471v2 . L2 certificates do not automatically bound peaks.
PLL/DC-loop interactions and their bandwidth design already exist: Zhang et al.,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05932 .
PLL transient Lyapunov/reset design already exists: Sun et al.,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00489 .
Therefore do not claim that gradients, graph Fourier transforms, PLL tuning, or
DAE models individually establish novelty. The specific co-design objective,
network-adjoint replacement value, falsifiable counterexample, and validated
frontier must together earn the contribution against those baselines.
