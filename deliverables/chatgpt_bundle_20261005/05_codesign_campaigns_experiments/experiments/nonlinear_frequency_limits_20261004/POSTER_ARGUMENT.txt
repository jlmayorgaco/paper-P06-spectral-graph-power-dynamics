WORKING TITLE
Beyond Nodal Damping: Network Limits on Local Frequency Support

ONE SENTENCE TO REMEMBER
For a declared nonlinear lossless network, two power-flow endpoints impose
a local-frequency inertia floor that moving the same inertia or adding
energy-neutral damping cannot remove.

THE QUESTION
Can a perfectly shaped average frequency hide a local violation that no
energy-neutral controller can eliminate at the available inertia?

THE ANSWER CURRENTLY PROVED
Yes. For a localized step, the exact secant graph gives
  m+kappa > d0^2 (e_k-c)^T Lbar_k^dagger (e_k-c)
as a necessary condition for all local frequencies to avoid crossing the
final value, with zero net supplementary energy. The worst area is at the
disturbed site, by a graph maximum principle; the worst instantaneous peak
need not be there.

In the dimensionless two-node instantaneous-damping example,
  inf m = 2 asin(P/2)/P.
The boundary is not attained. A closed-form controller supplies a feasible
trajectory for every m strictly above it, without actuator bounds. For
P=1,m=41/40, COI is exactly monotone but a local overshoot is unavoidable.
The uncontrolled case converges by a proved energy barrier and LaSalle.

THE GRAPH IS FUNCTIONAL, NOT DECORATIVE
The secant resistance is a sum of event projections weighted by inverse
graph eigenvalues, a minimum flow energy, and bounded below by every cut.
It measures transfer between the event site and distributed primary response.
For finite amplitude, it uses the TWO endpoints, not a frozen initial Jacobian.

CONTROLLER DESIGN CONSEQUENCE
The boundary identifies when waveform reshaping with zero net energy cannot
meet the specification. Escape requires changing total support, net energy,
primary response, network/endpoints, or the performance contract.
For the two-node ideal class, the construction is explicit. For detailed
GFL, the same conclusion requires a model-specific derivation and validation.
