Transitions:
Exact Collective Closure, Compatibility Geometry,

Jorge L. Mayorga Taborda
Universidad de los Andes, Bogota, Colombia
Abstract—Replacing synchronous generators (SGs) by grid-
following converters (GFLs) is often summarized by penetra-
tion, grid-strength, or fixed-composition stability metrics. This
manuscript develops a different planning object: an inclusion-
minimal SG-to-GFL replacement portfolio whose transverse
dynamics are unstable although every proper replacement subset is
stable. Starting from the full phasor-domain differential-algebraic
equations (DAEs), we derive an exact transverse quotient, a
dynamic multiport representation, and a determinant factorization
that separates local replacement factors from network-mediated
collective closure. At a simple collective boundary, a critical
eigenvalue of the interaction operator reaches −1, while every
blocker member sees the remainder through a contextual return
with eigenvalue +1. In the frozen IEEE 39-bus benchmark, all
15 proper subsets of {30, 33, 35, 37} are stable while the complete
portfolio has 𝛼⊥ = +0.1270 s−1 at 0.6223 Hz; physical local factors
remain regular while the complete collective singular value falls
to 1.26 × 10−8. We distinguish the complete-spectrum blocker
hypergraph H⊥ from a mode-scoped diagnostic hypergraph HΩ,
because a hard frequency window can change membership without
an imaginary-axis crossing. Controller policy reorganizes the
complete-spectrum blocker geometry, showing that compatibility
is not a scalar penetration number. Finally, we replace a singular
“Green function at the pole” by the critical resolvent residue
𝒢★
𝐻, derive the total pole sensitivity including power-flow re-
equilibration, and formulate blocker-conditioned line, bus, zone,
and hypergraph-aware repair scores. These repair equations
are derived here, but their blind 46-branch ranking experiment
remains a preregistered validation target rather than a claimed
result. The manuscript is a detailed theory and presentation
companion, not a universal stability or EMT certificate.

I. Motivation and Four-Result Narrative
T
HE central engineering question is not only how much
inverter-based generation a grid can host, but which
finite combinations of technology substitutions first become
dynamically incompatible. Existing small-signal, impedance,
grid-strength, critical-cluster, and stability-manifold methods
answer important neighboring questions [1], [3], [4], [6]–[8].
The present program asks instead:
Working technical companion manuscript for the IEEE IAS Annual Meeting
2026 poster development. It consolidates proved theory, frozen IEEE-39
evidence, and one explicitly labeled proposed extension for dynamic topology
repair.
Which inclusion-minimal SG-to-GFL replacement
sets fail, what exact dynamic feedback creates their
boundary, how does controller policy reorganize the
failing sets, and which control or topology actions
move the boundary most effectively?
The poster-oriented theory is organized around four results:
1) Minimal blocker: a complete replacement set can be
unstable although every proper subset is stable.
2) Exact collective closure: local device factors can remain
regular while a network-mediated interaction factor alone
becomes singular.
3) Compatibility geometry: the minimal incompatible sets
form a policy-dependent hypergraph, not a single penetration
threshold.
4) Diagnose-to-repair: the same dynamic operator yields exact
boundary derivatives and, after a critical Green-residue lift,
line/bus/zone repair fields.
The novelty position is intentionally narrow. Minimal failure
sets, Schur complements, generalized Nyquist/return ratios,
eigenvalue sensitivities, and resolvent residues are classical or
have direct prior use in power-system impedance participation
and remedial-action studies [5], [9]–[11]. The candidate con-
tribution is their synthesis around a counterfactual SG →GFL
replacement hypergraph: identify a minimal failing portfolio,
prove its collective-return anatomy, track how controller policy
reorganizes that discrete object, and design repairs that protect
the hypergraph rather than only one fixed composition.

design extension that still requires its dedicated branch-ranking
experiment.

XII. Topology Evidence and the Proposed Blind
Green-Residue Test
A frozen finite-action campaign already establishes that
topology can both repair and create incompatibility. At P4, four
single branch doublings (campaign indices 0, 1, 13, and 43)
independently drive the 𝐻4 abscissa negative while every proper
subset is already stable. Conversely, some outages create new
smaller blockers, including cases with 𝜅 = 1. Across 810 tested
policy/action pairs, the complete-spectrum blocker hypergraph
changes in 169 cases. Static changes in Fiedler value, Kirchhoff
index, gSCR, and minimum SCR did not consistently predict
the effect.
The next experiment should be preregistered before the finite
labels are consulted:
1) Freeze P4, 𝐻4, the equilibrium, and the critical root 𝑠★
𝐻.
2) Compute 𝑟𝐻, ℓ𝐻, 𝒢★
𝐻, and 𝜅NEP once.
3) For each admissible branch, compute both the direct/frozen
score and the total re-equilibrated score (129), preserving
the structural/flow/controller decomposition (126).
4) Rank branches without using finite doubling/outage labels.
5) Reveal the finite labels and report sign accuracy, Spearman
correlation with Δ𝛼⊥, recall@4 for the four known P4
repairs, and precision–recall AUC because the positive repair
class is sparse.
6) Re-evaluate the highest-ranked proposed repairs across
the complete protected portfolio family to test whether
a repaired blocker is replaced by a new smaller blocker.
The strongest possible outcome is not merely a high rank
correlation. It is a mechanistic separation: a static/direct
topology score fails, while the total Green-residue score
succeeds because power-flow redistribution and controller self-
energy materially change the line ranking. A negative result is
equally informative and should remain in the record without
retuning.

XV . Conclusion
The theory developed here separates four questions that are
often conflated in SG-to-GFL transition studies. The complete-
spectrum blocker hypergraph identifies which finite portfolios
first fail; the exact dynamic multiport and contextual return ex-
plain why a selected blocker closes collectively; the policy maps
show that compatibility is a controller-dependent combinatorial
geometry rather than a scalar penetration threshold; and the
critical Green residue provides a mathematically well-defined
bridge from the singular mode to spatial repair sensitivities.
The most important pending experiment is deliberately narrow:
rank the frozen IEEE-39 branch actions from one blocker mode
using the total re-equilibrated residue score, reveal the known
finite repair labels only afterward, and verify that the selected
action repairs the protected hypergraph rather than merely
one eigenvalue. Success would provide a compact diagnose–
localize–repair chain; failure would sharply delimit the value
of modal repair fields. In either case, complete-spectrum labels,
model scope, and the distinction between classical algebra and
new interpretation must remain explicit.
