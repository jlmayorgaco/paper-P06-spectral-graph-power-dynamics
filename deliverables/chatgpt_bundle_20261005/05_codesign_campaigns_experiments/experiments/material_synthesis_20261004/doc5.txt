Connected Intervention Calculus for Converter-Rich
Power Systems:
Re-Equilibrated Dynamic Externalities, Cycle
Mechanisms, and Certified EMT Boundaries
Jorge L. Mayorga Taborda
Abstract—Converter-rich power systems expose a planning
problem that is not captured by asking whether each project,
controller, or line is acceptable in isolation: finite engineering
interventions can become harmful only after the nonlinear
operating point is re-solved and the resulting network–controller
feedback closes across several actions. This paper develops a con-
nected intervention calculus for that problem. The mathematical
foundation is rebuilt on the equilibrium manifold rather than on
a frozen low-rank perturbation. If H(y, a) = 0 defines the AC
operating point and T (s, a) is the corresponding re-linearized
dynamic pencil, the total action derivative contains both direct
and equilibrium-mediated channels. An exact Jacobi transport
identity then evaluates finite spectral change along any regular
action path without assuming that the final perturbation is low
rank.
The logarithm of the determinant is used as a connected
generating functional. Mixed action derivatives obey a partition-
cycle formula: higher-order re-equilibration terms appear as multi-
action vertices, resolvents transport them through the network,
and traces close the result into connected hypercycles. In the affine
fixed-equilibrium limit, the formula collapses exactly to oriented
controller-mediated action cycles. A second exact result shows
that every finite Harsanyi/M ¨obius externality is the integral of the
corresponding connected mixed derivative over the physical action
cell. This unifies local differential interaction and finite portfolio
non-additivity while keeping engineering allocation real-valued
and branch free.
For low-rank port actions, the general theory reduces to a
MIMO action quiver. Determinants are expanded exactly through
finite cycle systems; logarithms are organized by closed walks
and connected overlapping-cycle clusters, with recent quiver
trace identities providing the proper prime-cyclic-walk language.
Holonomy spectra are invariant to local proprietary state
coordinates, and the algebra generated by fundamental holonomies
determines whether a common scalar-channel representation exists.
Certified sparsification is tied to decay of sparse resolvents rather
than arbitrary edge thresholding.
The same operator structure produces a physically meaningful
model-fidelity problem. A dangerous action core is mapped to a
physical EMT region by preserving the exterior Schur self-energy
seen at its boundary. The proposed minimal detailed region is the
smallest region whose reduced exterior self-energy remains within
a prescribed frequency-domain/structured-robustness tolerance.
Complex-frequency strength, QSS frequency, and sinusoidality are
retained as local temporal/waveform diagnostics; structured EMT-
model uncertainty supplies an independent trust gate. Wavemaker

I. I NTRODUCTION
C
ONVERTER-RICH grids have made a familiar engineer-
ing workflow increasingly fragile. A candidate line is
screened, a controller is tuned, a BESS is added, and each
modification is judged individually. Yet the implemented system
is not the sum of those isolated studies: every finite change can
move the nonlinear AC equilibrium, modify converter operating
points, reshape network transfer paths, and close feedback
through other devices. The relevant question is therefore not
merely “which bus is weak?” but
When do finite engineering actions fail to compose,
which connected network–controller mechanism cre-
ates the non-additive effect, and what is the smallest
model/control change that removes it?
The question sits between several mature tool families.
Eigenvalue and participation analysis identify modes and states;
impedance/return-ratio and multiport dissipativity methods
diagnose converter coupling; graph and grid-strength metrics
characterize local/network support; complex frequency provides
a physically meaningful magnitude–angle dynamical coordi-
nate; stability-manifold methods map admissible controller
regions; EMT simulation resolves dynamics omitted by phasor
models. None of these should be portrayed as deficient. The
narrower gap pursued here is finite portfolio composition :
the joint effect of named engineering actions after the actual
operating point is re-solved, and the mapping of that mechanism
to the smallest detailed model that must preserve it.

That distinction matters industrially. Recent work on

planning need is consequently not another scalar replacement
for EMT, but a mechanistic triage layer that can say which
interaction must survive the detailed model.

B. Main contributions
The paper makes five tightly connected contributions.
1) Re-equilibrated finite-action calculus. The operating point
is treated as an implicit equilibrium manifold. Exact total
action derivatives and a finite spectral-transport identity
remain valid when a physical intervention changes the entire
re-linearized operator.
2) Connected interaction theorem. Mixed derivatives of
log detT are written as a partition-cycle expansion. In the
affine limit they become exact closed action-cycle traces;
finite Harsanyi/M¨obius externalities are exact integrals of
these connected densities.
3) Cycle/cluster and MIMO structure. The determinant is
separated into finite cycle-cover physics and connected
closed-walk/cluster physics. MIMO action holonomies are
organized by a coordinate-invariant holonomy algebra that
determines common scalarizability.
4) Certified localization. Sparse-resolvent decay motivates
frequency-dependent action sparsification with perturbation
certificates, addressing the otherwise serious possibility of
one giant dense SCC.
5) Cycle-preserving EMT boundary. A dangerous interaction
core is mapped to a physical detailed region by preserving
the Schur self-energy of the omitted exterior, combined
with waveform and structured model-mismatch trust gates.
The mathematical identities are not presented as new linear
algebra in isolation. The candidate power-systems novelty is
their composition into a finite-intervention framework and the
specific claim that connected dynamic externalities can be
localized, attributed, and preserved across RMS-to-EMT model
boundaries.

The distinction is the object to which the return-ratio algebra
is applied. Conventional impedance/return-ratio analysis asks
whether one assembled configuration is stable and which
devices/control loops participate in the problematic loop. The
connected intervention calculus introduces physical action
coordinates, pulls the entire dynamic operator back to the
re-equilibrated AC operating manifold, and asks whether finite
named actions compose. The mixed derivatives and finite
M¨obius dividends then quantify irreducible project-set effects
that are not obtained by analyzing only the final configuration.
This distinction is especially clear for three actions. A
conventional final-configuration loop matrix can diagnose an
unstable mode in the joint system. The connected calculus
additionally tests whether every singleton and pair was accept-
able, computes the genuine triple dividend, and separates that
dividend into re-equilibrium vertices and pure feedback returns
by Theorem 7. The final loop diagnosis and the portfolio-
composition diagnosis are complementary, not competing,
analyses.
A. What the new cycle language adds and what it does not
Impedance methods already expose closed converter–grid
feedback and can suggest retuning. Extended impedance modal
analysis can separate PLL- and current-loop contributions [64],
while recent Pauli decompositions seek a more interpretable
physical basis for MIMO impedance coupling [65]. Accord-
ingly, phrases such as “existing methods cannot identify
controller interactions” are excluded from this manuscript.
The new cycle language is used for three narrower purposes:
(i) to label intervention cycles rather than only device-port
loops, (ii) to organize higher-order finite non-additivity through
connected cycle clusters and dividends, and (iii) to link the
identified action core to an exterior-self-energy model boundary.
If an impedance/dissipativity method provides the same final-
config root cause more directly, it should be used as a baseline
and its diagnosis should agree with the relevant connected core.

VII. C ONNECTED INTERVENTION CALCULUS ON THE
RE-E QUILIBRATED OPERATING MANIFOLD
The central revision of this manuscript is conceptual rather
than cosmetic. A physical power-system intervention is not, in
general, a low-rank perturbation of a frozen linear model. A line
addition, point-of-interconnection change, controller retuning,
or converter replacement changes the nonlinear equilibrium
and therefore changes the entire linearized descriptor operator.
The correct exact object is consequently the pullback of the
dynamic pencil to the equilibrium manifold . Low-rank action
matrices and action quivers then appear as a useful special
case, not as the foundation.

tangent-cycle terms of Corollary 5. They are local mechanism
laws, not a claim that the full re-equilibrated finite intervention
obeys the same power law without integration along the
operating manifold.
An initially attractive hypothesis was that lower inertia
always makes action portfolios more non-composable. The
experiments falsified that statement. The direction depends on
what happens to damping at the same time.
Consider a dominant isolated network mode with modal
mass mk, damping dk, and stiffness λk. Under uniform co-
retirement
Mα = αM0, D α = αD0, (126)
its natural frequency scales as ωk ∝ α−1/2. At s = j ωk,
the inertial and stiffness terms cancel in the modal dynamic
stiffness and
|tk(jωk)| ∝ α1/2, |Gk(jωk)| ∝ α−1/2. (127)
A closed action cycle of order p containing p Green factors
therefore obeys
|K(p)| ∝ α−p/2. (128)
Pair and triple cycles scale as α−1 and α−3/2.
If inertia is reduced while absolute damping is preserved ,
Dα = D0, the damping term scales oppositely at the resonance
frequency, yielding
|Gk| ∝ α+1/2, |K(p)| ∝ α+p/2. (129)

controller retuning may therefore leave the size of one loop
nearly unchanged while rotating its return direction so that
it no longer reinforces another loop. Non-Abelian holonomy
makes that mode-mixing mechanism visible.

A. Relation to impedance and robust multi-vendor stability
Multiport impedance and generalized Nyquist methods
already provide rigorous ways to expose converter interactions,
while robust-stability work explicitly addresses uncertain
multi-vendor controls [43]. The action-quiver contribution
is orthogonal: its vertices are not required to be individual
physical converters. A vertex can be a proposed line, an
alternate POI, a controller-mode change, or a whole project. The
determinant restriction property then asks a planning question
that impedance analysis alone does not automatically answer:
which finite set of engineering decisions must coexist before
the harmful closed path exists?
Conversely, the quiver should use impedance/EMT infor-
mation whenever it is the better port model. Nothing in
the determinant construction requires an electromechanical
state-space realization. An identified or frequency-scanned
MIMO return map can supply the edge transports, provided the
action restriction and base-model assumptions are documented.
This makes the framework a possible organizing layer over
heterogeneous vendor models rather than a competitor to them.
B. Relation to eigenvalue sensitivity and participation
Participation factors answer which states or devices par-
ticipate in a mode; eigenvalue sensitivities answer how an
infinitesimal parameter change moves that mode. The action
calculus asks a different counterfactual: if several declared finite
modifications are applied, does the characteristic correction
factorize into isolated effects or does a closed cross-action
feedback core appear? The two views are complementary. In
fact, the full nonlinear-eigenvalue sensitivity of Section IV can
rank candidate action variables within a cycle after the cycle
is located.
C. Why the gap is especially acute in cluster interconnection
A serial study can often focus on one project’s incremental
impact against a fixed prior network. Cluster studies intention-
ally evaluate many projects and network upgrades together. As
the number of power-electronic projects grows, the number of
possible controller/topology combinations grows much faster
than the number of projects. The SCC and cycle representation
is valuable precisely because it seeks structural zeros in
that combinatorial space: projects outside the same directed
feedback core have zero determinant interaction under the
model, while only the small closed core requires combinatorial
attention.

XXVII. V ALIDATION ARCHITECTURE AND FALSIFICATION
PROGRAM
A theory paper must distinguish exact identities from
engineering validation. The validation ladder is therefore
organized around falsifiable questions.
A. Gate A: re-equilibrated identities
For every finite action campaign, recompute the nonlinear AC
equilibrium and audit Theorem 3 by numerical path integration.
Compare finite M ¨obius differences against the integral of
mixed connected derivatives in Theorem 6. A frozen low-
rank approximation is reported separately so its breakdown is
visible rather than hidden.
B. Gate B: closest strong baselines
The flagship “strong grid–weak interaction core” experiment
must compare against SCR/gSCR-type quantities, CF strength
S(0:2), full modal/impedance information, dissipativity or
another robust multiport certificate, and ordinary eigenvalue
sensitivity. Fiedler connectivity remains a useful visualization
but is no longer treated as the main adversary.
C. Gate C: scalability and locality
For 10–100+ candidate actions, report raw action-graph den-
sity, certified pruned density, SCC size distribution, number/size
of connected cycle clusters, resolvent solves, runtime, and error
in the finite interaction observable. If the certified graph remains
one giant SCC, the claimed combinatorial scaling benefit must
be withdrawn for that regime.
D. Gate D: physical boundary selection
A detailed EMT or validated average-value model provides
the reference. Compare at least four region-selection rules: fixed
k-hop, electrical distance, strength-based selection, and the
self-energy-preserving criterion (106). Report error in critical
mode/frequency, peak current/voltage, protection-relevant quan-
tities, and runtime versus retained model size. The mechanism-
preserving method succeeds only if it attains a better accuracy–
cost Pareto frontier, not merely if it identifies a plausible region.
E. Gate E: cycle surgery
For every strong mechanism claim, modify an action on the
predicted connected core and a matched action outside it. The
method should predict the sign and approximate magnitude of
the modal/security change. A negative control outside the core
is necessary before using strong mechanistic language.
F . Modern external benchmark
The real 23-Hz large-electronic-load event provides an
unusually relevant external target: oscillations emerged near 320
MW and detailed EMT reproduced the recorded behavior [59].
A future implementation should test whether the connected-
action/self-energy machinery localizes the grid–load feedback
and whether the post-event controller/firmware change can be
represented as a recovery action. The present manuscript cites
this event as a target, not as an internally reproduced result.

XXVIII. F ALSIFICATION -FIRST VALIDATION PROGRAM
FOR A TRANSACTIONS SUBMISSION
The mathematical reconstruction is only useful if its strongest
claims can fail cleanly. The validation program is therefore
organized around comparisons that would disprove, rather than
merely illustrate, the proposed mechanism.
A. Gate A: re-equilibrated calculus versus frozen low-rank
surrogates
For each finite line/controller/POI action, the nonlinear AC
equilibrium is re-solved. Three predictions are then compared:
1) the exact endpoint change from the re-linearized full model;
2) the path integral in Theorem 3;
3) the frozen affine/quiver approximation and its adequacy
bound (52).
The theory fails this gate if the exact path integral does not
reproduce the endpoint determinant change numerically, or if
the proposed curvature bound fails to cover the frozen-model
error. A useful result is not that the affine approximation is
always bad; it is that the gate predicts when it is safe to use.
B. Gate B: mechanism identification against strong baselines
The principal comparison should not use Fiedler connectivity
alone. For the same operating points and actions, the study
should report conventional SCR/gSCR where defined, the
zero/first/second-order complex-frequency strength indicators,
modal/impedance participation, a multiport dissipativity or
return-ratio diagnosis, and the connected action quantities.
The claim is supported only if the connected calculus adds
information unavailable from the competing diagnosis: finite
portfolio non-additivity, a higher-order action set, or a distinct
minimum recovery action. Existing impedance/dissipativity
methods must be credited whenever they identify the same
root cause.
C. Gate C: cycle surgery and negative controls
A candidate mechanism C ⋆ or connected SCC is accepted
only after intervention surgery. The experiment perturbs one
member of the predicted core, recomputes the full model, and
checks the predicted change in damping/contour reserve. A
matched perturbation of comparable engineering magnitude
outside the identified core acts as a negative control. Repeating
this across operating points distinguishes a mechanism from a
post-hoc participation map.
D. Gate D: scalability and certified locality
For increasing numbers of candidate actions, report the
unpruned action density, decay of port transports with physical
distance, nonnormality/conditioning factors, certified pruning
error, SCC-size distribution, number of connected cycle clusters
retained, sparse linear-solve count, and total runtime. If the
resolvent becomes global, the method must report a large
core rather than forcing a favorable complexity result. A
strong scaling figure should compare exhaustive 2m coalition
evaluation with the connected/SCC strategy on cases where
exact enumeration is still possible.
E. Gate E: model boundary versus full electromagnetic refer-
ence
The industrial headline requires an electromagnetic reference.
The full detailed model is treated as ground truth for the
tested model family. Candidate detailed regions are selected
by (i) k-hop distance, (ii) electrical distance, (iii) strength-
based heuristics, and (iv) the exterior-self-energy certificate.
Each boundary is evaluated on critical mode count, oscil-
lation frequency/damping, terminal transfer functions, peak
current/voltage, limit activation, and runtime. The proposed
method is useful only if it achieves a better fidelity–cost Pareto
frontier, not merely a smaller region.
F . Gate F: a second modern-grid benchmark
The IEEE 39-bus mechanism is necessary but insuffi-
cient. A second benchmark should involve a large elec-
tronic/computational load or a multi-vendor converter clus-
ter. The recent 23-Hz large-electronic-load case provides
a particularly relevant target [59]: the method should test
whether the observed frequency band is associated with a
compact connected action core, whether local strength remains
a poor proxy for that mechanism, and whether the self-
energy boundary predicts which surrounding dynamics must
be retained. Failure to recover a meaningful core would narrow
the claimed industrial scope.
This validation architecture is intentionally demanding. The
present reduced IEEE 39-bus studies provide mechanism
evidence, not completion of Gates E–F. The reconstructed
theory is designed so that the missing electromagnetic evidence
is a clear external experiment, not a hidden assumption inside
the mathematics.
XXIX. D ISCUSSION
A. Why the action-space view is the main conceptual shift
Most traditional grid metrics assign a property to a node,
line, or mode. The intervention formulation instead asks about
a relation among candidate decisions . This is not a semantic
change. A line reinforcement can have a positive isolated
margin, a controller retune can have a positive isolated margin,
and yet the pair can close a high-gain feedback cycle. No scalar
score attached separately to the line and controller can encode
that all-order relation unless it is recomputed jointly.
The action-space determinant also separates two reasons a
cheap planning workflow can fail. First, the base model may

self-energy/fidelity problem. Second, even a correct G0 may
be used incorrectly if actions are ranked independently despite
strong off-diagonal KA. That is the composability problem.
Figure 1 separates the model-fidelity and action-composability
levels because fixing one does not fix the other.

D. What makes the attribution result unusually valuable
The prime-cycle Shapley result is potentially the most
distinctive mathematical bridge in the project because it
converts a generic computational problem into a structured
one. General Shapley evaluation is expensive because every
coalition can carry an independent value. Here the characteristic
function derives from a perturbation determinant with principal-
submatrix restriction. Pure synergies therefore live on feedback
cycles and can be enumerated locally in SCCs. The power
system supplies the Green operator; graph-cycle algebra
supplies the decomposition; cooperative-game theory supplies
the fair-allocation axioms. The combination is more than
a juxtaposition because the determinant identifies the game
dividends exactly.
E. Limits of causal language
The word “causal” should be used in an engineering,
intervention-defined sense: execute a declared set of finite
actions against a common base, and attribute the resulting
spectral interaction to closed action cycles. The framework does
not infer causal structure from observational data, nor does it
identify market/legal liability. It provides counterfactual but-
for structure within a specified physical model. That boundary
should remain explicit in any journal or regulatory presentation.
XXX. C ONCLUSION
This paper replaces a frozen low-rank intervention picture
by a connected calculus on the re-equilibrated operating
manifold. The key result is structural: finite power-system
actions can be studied through mixed connected derivatives
of a physical pullback pencil, and in the affine/low-rank limit
those derivatives reduce exactly to controller-mediated action
cycles. Finite Harsanyi/M ¨obius externalities are integrals of
the same connected densities, so differential interaction, finite
portfolio non-additivity, and real-valued attribution belong to
one theory rather than to separate patches.
The reconstruction also clarifies the role of cycle language.
Determinants involve finite disjoint cycle systems; logarithms
involve closed walks and connected overlapping-cycle clusters;
MIMO cycle holonomies are meaningful through similarity
invariants and the algebra they generate. Certified locality
is required before SCC compression is trusted. Complex-
frequency strength remains a local temporal diagnostic, not a
surrogate for global interaction closure.
For power systems, the most important consequence is the
model-boundary formulation. Once a dangerous connected core
is identified, the exterior network should be reduced only insofar
as its Schur self-energy at the core boundary is preserved.
This converts EMT study-area selection from a geographic
heuristic into a mechanism-preservation problem that can be
combined with structured model-mismatch certificates. The
IEEE 39-bus studies demonstrate the motivating non-additivity
and connection-design mechanism at reduced-model fidelity;
decisive industrial claims require the validation gates stated
explicitly in Section XXVII.
The intended contribution is therefore narrow but potentially
consequential: determine when finite engineering interventions
fail to compose, expose the connected controller–network
mechanism responsible, preserve that mechanism in the smallest
credible detailed model, and compute the minimum physically
realizable path back to a required security margin.
