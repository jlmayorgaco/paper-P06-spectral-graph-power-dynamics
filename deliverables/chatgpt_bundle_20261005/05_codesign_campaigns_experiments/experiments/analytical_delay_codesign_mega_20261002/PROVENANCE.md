# Provenance and immutable scientific contract

This directory is the sole output location for the Mega experiment. Previous experiment directories and reports are read-only inputs. `MEGA_EXPERIMENT_MANIFEST.json` records git HEAD, dirty state, source hashes, Julia/Python and package versions, event/solver tolerances, frozen gain bounds, delay-pattern hash, and RNG seed.

The full nonlinear IEEE-39 PowerDynamics architecture comes from `experiments/nonlinear_codesign_20261001/PDPhysicalReference.jl`; initialized design, descriptor, and power sharing from `src/bnd_model_expN/PDExactDesignN.jl`; delayed PLL characteristic from `experiments/delay_dressed_replacement_frontier_20261002/DelayCharacteristic.jl`. The five events are bus 8 at -100 MW, bus 16 at +100/-100 MW, and bus 29 at +100/-100 MW. They occur at t=1 s and are simulated to t=61 s, with 0.5 s causal phase-difference frequency and RoCoF observed at all 39 buses. Limits: |Delta f|<=0.5 Hz, |RoCoF|<=0.5 Hz/s, voltage 0.9–1.1 pu, spectral real part <=-0.05 s^-1, normalized SG actuator slack >=0.002. The declared current/energy hard limits are not certified by this model.

The GFL candidate ports are buses 30–39, with original initialized generator dispatch as the replacement MW weight. The PLL gains have nominal Kp=10*pi and Ki=(10*pi)^2/4, bounded to 0.25–4 times nominal. Fixed PLL delays are exogenous, inserted only on the shared scalar phase-error measurement. `FROZEN_DELAY_PATTERNS.json` in the previous delay experiment contains the preregistered heterogeneous assignments and SHA-256 multiset identity; it is never optimized in this experiment.

M0 re-executes a local copy of the prior physical validator and action-space checks in this new directory, then compares their outputs against frozen prior results. A separate `m0_recheck_zero_schur.jl` repeats the gauge-deflated hidden-block condition diagnosis. Re-execution of copied code establishes run parity, while M2's random designs and determinant checks test extrapolation of the algebraic action identity. Neither procedure by itself certifies a nonlinear/global optimum.
