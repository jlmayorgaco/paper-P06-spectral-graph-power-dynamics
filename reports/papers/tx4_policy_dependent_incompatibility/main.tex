\begin{abstract}
Replacing synchronous generators (SGs) by grid-following inverters is planned
unit by unit, yet small-signal stability is not additive: every subset of a
portfolio can be stable while the portfolio is not. We formulate this as a minimal
incompatibility hypergraph over binary replacement decisions and study it on
a nonlinear phasor differential--algebraic model. Because frozen benchmark
models lack primary frequency control, stability is posed exactly in transverse
coordinates, as a quotient by the rotation and common-frequency-drift center
subspace, without discarding small eigenvalues. We prove that the hypergraph
changes only where a transverse eigenvalue crosses the imaginary axis, that it
characterizes the portfolios that are safe in every implementation order, and
that all non-additivity of the characteristic function is the principal-minor
expansion of a network-closure operator between the replaced buses. A
symmetry-deflated port extends the determinant test to zero-frequency
crossings. Seven preregistered synthetic Monte Carlo suites and four suites on
the IEEE 39-bus system test every statement: exact nonlinear symmetries, 80/80
time-domain verdicts, and a zero-frequency port holdout. One IEEE-39 rule failed
as implemented and is reported with its post-hoc correction. On IEEE-39, the converter reactive policy changes which coalitions
fail: 30, 36, and 16 distinct hypergraphs appear on three policy planes. A
2096.6-MW (4270.7-MVA) four-unit portfolio is unstable although every proper
subset is stable, and each of ten frozen boundaries is closed only by the
full-order network interaction of the changing coalition. With the source's
documented turbine governors the structure persists in a smaller region,
while the four-unit coalition is stabilized.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{G}{eneration} portfolios are decarbonized by replacing
synchronous generators (SGs) with inverter-based resources, typically plant by
plant. Small-signal stability of the resulting system is, however, a property
of the \emph{portfolio}. Low-inertia and converter-driven instabilities are now
part of the stability taxonomy \cite{hatziargyriou2021definition,
milano2018lowinertia, markovic2021lowinertia}, and screening indices such as
short-circuit ratios, single-port impedance criteria, or generalized short-circuit
ratios \cite{sun2011impedance, harnefors2007admittance, wang2019harmonic,
xin2016gscr, nerc2017lowstrength} evaluate a configuration, not the
combinatorial structure of a replacement plan. A planner needs a different
answer: \emph{which minimal groups of replacements are mutually incompatible,
and how does that set depend on the converter control policy?}

This paper studies that object. For a candidate set of $m$ SGs and a converter
policy $\theta$, the minimal incompatibility hypergraph $\Hy(\theta)$ collects
the inclusion-minimal replacement portfolios that are unstable. Its minimum
edge size $\kappa(\theta)$ is the smallest incompatible coalition. Two
technical obstacles appear immediately. First, frozen benchmark models
(IEEE-39, Kundur, IEEE-68) have no rotor damping and no governors; their
linearizations carry an exact double zero eigenvalue, so ``stable'' is
undefined unless the neutral directions are handled exactly rather than by a
magnitude cutoff. Second, a label per portfolio says nothing about \emph{why}
a coalition fails when its subsets do not.

\emph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A transverse stability notion (Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse}): the
rotation and common-frequency drift span an exactly invariant Jordan center
subspace; the quotient is exact for the linearization and for the nonlinear
reduced field, so no eigenvalue is discarded by magnitude.
\item Structural properties of $\Hy(\theta)$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:structure}
and~\ref{prop:hierarchy}, Theorem~\ref{thm:localization}): antichain,
boundary localization at imaginary-axis crossings, and the equivalence between
``hyperedge-free'' and ``safe in every implementation order''; a monotone
tractable class (Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone}); and the credited NP-hardness of
minimum-cardinality destabilization (Proposition~\ref{prop:complexity}).
\item A network-closure factorization of the characteristic function
(Theorem~\ref{thm:factorization}): on an exact descriptor realization that is
affine in the replacement indicators, all non-additivity is a principal-minor
expansion of an interaction operator $Q$ generated by the network between the
replaced buses; boundaries are zeros of $\det(I+Q_{SS})$, and their motion
follows an eigenvalue-derivative formula (Proposition~\ref{prop:gradient}).
A symmetry-deflated port (Theorem~\ref{thm:port}) extends the determinant test
to crossings through $s=0$.
\item Preregistered Monte Carlo validation: seven synthetic suites built to
satisfy each theorem's hypotheses, and four suites over random policies and
portfolios of the IEEE 39-bus system, including nonlinear time-domain
simulation (TDS).
\item IEEE-39 evidence: policy-dependent witness structure, the anatomy of ten
frozen boundaries, robustness to documented governors, and planning
consequences, with every result stated under its model scope.
\end{enumerate}

\emph{What is not claimed.} The eigenvalue test (generalized Nyquist
\cite{macfarlane1970return, macfarlane1977gnc}), Schur complements and
Sylvester's determinant identity \cite{horn2012matrix}, M\"obius inversion
\cite{rota1964mobius}, clutters and minimal non-faces
\cite{stanley1996combinatorics}, minimal failure sets in $N\!-\!k$ analysis
\cite{bienstock2010nk}, and positive-systems monotonicity
\cite{farina2000positive} are classical. The complexity statement is a corollary
of the sparse-PCA reduction \cite{magdon2017sparsepca}. The contribution is
the object $\Hy(\theta)$, its exact transverse definition, its
network-closure anatomy, and the measured policy dependence.

\section{Model and Problem Formulation}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Phasor DAE}
The system is the index-one DAE
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dae}
\dot x = f(x,z;\delta,\theta),\qquad 0 = g(x,z;\delta,\theta),
\end{equation}
where $x\in\RR^{n}$ collects device states, $z\in\RR^{2N}$ the rectangular bus
voltages, and $g$ is Kirchhoff's current law with the bus admittance matrix,
device injections, and constant-power loads. Index one means that $g_z$ is
nonsingular along the trajectories of interest. At an equilibrium the reduced
Jacobian is $A=f_x-f_z g_z^{-1} g_x$.

\emph{Synchronous generators.} Each SG uses a two-axis model on its own base
\cite{sauer1998}. With $(v_d,v_q)$ the terminal voltage in the rotor frame and
$(i_d,i_q)$ obtained from the stator equations with transient reactances
$x'_d,x'_q$,
\begin{align}
\dot\delta&=\omega_B(\omega-1),\qquad
M\dot\omega=P_m-P_e-D(\omega-1),\label{eq:swing}\\
T'_{d0}\dot e'_q&=e_{fd}-e'_q-(x_d-x'_d)i_d,\nonumber\\
T'_{q0}\dot e'_d&=-e'_d+(x_q-x'_q)i_q,\nonumber
\end{align}
with air-gap power $P_e=v_di_d+v_qi_q+r_a(i_d^2+i_q^2)$. The excitation is a
first-order AVR $T_E\dot e_{fd}=K_A(v_{\rm ref}+v_s-|V|)-e_{fd}$, and the
stabilizer is the power-input washout-and-lag
$v_s=K_S x_6$, $T_4\dot x_6=T_5(P_e-x_5)/T_6-x_6$, $T_6\dot x_5=P_e-x_5$. As in
the source data, $D=0$ and $P_m$ is constant (no governor).

\emph{Grid-following inverters.} Each GFL has a synchronous-reference-frame
PLL, filtered power measurements, PI outer loops, and PI inner current loops
behind a filter reactance $x_f$:
\begin{align}
\dot\vartheta&=k_{p,\rm pll}v_q+x_{\rm pll},\qquad \dot x_{\rm pll}=k_{i,\rm pll}v_q,
\label{eq:pll}\\
\tau\dot p_f&=P-p_f,\qquad \tau\dot q_f=Q-q_f,\nonumber\\
i_d^{\rm ref}&=k_p(p_{\rm ref}-p_f)+x_p,\quad
i_q^{\rm ref}=-k_q(q_{\rm cmd}-q_f)-x_q,\nonumber\\
\dot i_{dq}&=\tfrac{\omega_B}{x_f}\big(k_i(i_{dq}^{\rm ref}-i_{dq})+x_{i,dq}-r_fi_{dq}\big),
\nonumber
\end{align}
with PI states $\dot x_p=k_{ip}(p_{\rm ref}-p_f)$,
$\dot x_q=k_{iq}(q_{\rm cmd}-q_f)$, and $\dot x_{i,dq}=k_{ii}(i^{\rm ref}_{dq}-i_{dq})$.
The injection is $\gamma(i_d+ji_q)e^{j\vartheta}$, with $\gamma$ the rating
weight. The reactive command comes from a leaky Q/V regulator
\begin{align}
q_{\rm cmd}&=k_{p,v}\,g\,e+x_v,\qquad e=v_{\rm ref}-|V|,\label{eq:qv}\\
\dot x_v&= k_{i,v}\,g\,e-w\,(x_v-q_{\rm ref}),\nonumber
\end{align}
with leak $w=0.05$~rad/s; $g=0$ is exact fixed-$Q$ operation. The parameters
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:param}. Converter limits, DC links, and modulation
delay are not modeled, so nonlinear results are bounded by declared
model-scope guards (Section~\ref{sec:nonlinear}).

\subsection{Replacement portfolios and policy}
Let $V=\{1,\dots,m\}$ be the candidate SGs. A portfolio $S\subseteq V$ has
indicator $\delta_S\in\{0,1\}^m$. Replacing unit $i$ installs a GFL with the
same active and reactive dispatch (\emph{matched dispatch}) and rating equal to
the SG rating, so the AC power flow is identical for every portfolio. The
policy $\theta=(g,k,t,h)$ collects the Q/V gain $g$, a common scale $k$ of every
surviving SG's AVR gain, a scale $t$ of every AVR time constant, and a
heterogeneity exponent $h$ ($T_{E,i}\!\mapsto\! G\,(T_{E,i}/G)^h$, with $G$ the
geometric mean). Active dispatch (MW) and converter rating (MVA) are always
reported separately.

\section{Transverse Stability}\label{sec:transverse}

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:sym}
(i) Every device angle state (rotor angle, PLL angle) enters \eqref{eq:dae}
only through $V e^{-j\,\cdot}$, the other device states are written in their
own rotating frames, and loads and network are rotation-covariant.
(ii) Every SG has $D=0$ and constant $P_m$, and the network is
frequency-independent.
\end{assumption}

Let $R_x$ equal $1$ on every rotor and PLL angle and $0$ elsewhere, and let $w$
be the uniform frequency shift (speed $+1$, PLL integrator $+\omega_B$).

\begin{theorem}[Exact transverse quotient]\label{thm:transverse}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sym}(i), $A R_x=0$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sym}(i)--(ii),
$A w=\omega_B R_x$, so $C=\spanop\{R_x,w\}$ is $A$-invariant with
$A[R_x\ w]=[R_x\ w]J$, $J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&\omega_B\\0&0
\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ (a Jordan chain). For any orthonormal $Z$ with
$\mathrm{range}\,Z=C^\perp$ and $\Aperp=Z^\top A Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detid}
\det(sI-A)=s^2\det(sI-\Aperp),
\end{equation}
and $\sigma(\Aperp)$ does not depend on $Z$. Moreover, the reduced field
$r(x)=f(x,\psi(x))$, with $\psi$ the local algebraic solution, satisfies
$r(x+\phi R_x)=r(x)$ and $r(x+c\,w)=r(x)+c\,\omega_B R_x$ for all $\phi,c$. Hence
$\dot y = Z^\top r(x^*+Zy)$ is an exact reduced system whose linearization is
$\Aperp$. If some SG has $D\neq 0$ or a governor, only $C=\spanop\{R_x\}$ remains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Rotation covariance gives $f(x+\phi R_x,e^{j\phi}z)=f(x,z)$ and a covariant
$g$. Differentiating at $\phi=0$ gives $f_xR_x+f_zR_z=0$ and $g_xR_x+g_zR_z=0$
with $R_z=jV$, so $AR_x=0$. Under (ii), $x_0+c\,w+c\,\omega_B tR_x$ with
$z(t)=e^{jc\omega_B t}z_0$ solves \eqref{eq:dae} exactly (electrical power is
rotation invariant and the swing equation has $\dot\omega=0$ at speed $1+c$).
Differentiating in $c$ gives $Aw=\omega_BR_x$. In the basis $[U\ Z]$ with $U$
an orthonormal basis of $C$, invariance gives $Z^\top AU=0$, so $A$ is block
upper triangular with diagonal blocks $U^\top AU$ (spectrum $\{0,0\}$) and
$\Aperp$; this yields \eqref{eq:detid}. A different complement $ZO$ gives
$O^\top\Aperp O$. The nonlinear identities follow from the same invariances
applied to $f(x,\psi(x))$ with $\psi$ rotating covariantly and unaffected by
the uniform speed shift. Hence $Z^\top r(x+v)=Z^\top r(x)$ for $v\in C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}\label{def:stable}
A portfolio is \emph{transversely stable} at $\theta$ if
$\aperp(S,\theta)=\max\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(\Aperp(S,\theta))<0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The rotation is a gauge. The drift is a physical consequence of absent primary
frequency control, and transverse stability is stability modulo absolute
frequency restoration. A rule of the form ``ignore $|\lambda|\le\varepsilon$''
coincides with Definition~\ref{def:stable} only when $\Aperp$ has no physical
eigenvalue in that disc. Suite S1 (Section~\ref{sec:mc}) shows that it
miscounts every draw with a planted \emph{positive} physical mode of size
$10^{-5}$--$5\!\times\!10^{-4}$ (235 of 235) and none of the others.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Numerical classification of transverse labels}\label{sec:classify}
Every label in this paper is produced by the procedure below. It has three
outcomes and no magnitude cutoff.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Solve the AC equilibrium (Newton, residual $<10^{-12}$) and form the
DAE Jacobians by central differences at two step scales, $h$ and $2h$. Their
difference gives the Jacobian error $E$.
\item Build $R_x$ and $w$ from the device frames, verify $\|AR_x\|$ and
$\|Aw-\omega_BR_x\|$, delete identically zero rows (switched-off services),
and form $\Aperp=Z^\top AZ$ and $E_\perp=Z^\top EZ$.
\item If $\min|\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(\Aperp)|>2\times10^{-3}$~s$^{-1}$, the label
is the sign of $\aperp$.
\item Otherwise, compute the distance to the imaginary axis,
$d=\min_\omega\sigma_{\min}(\Aperp-j\omega I)$, after balancing. The label is
exact if $d>10\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon=\|E_\perp\|+$ backward error.
If not, the point is \textsc{boundary-or-unresolved} and is reported, never
assigned.
\end{enumerate}
The criterion in step 4 is numerical, not an interval certificate. Because
$\Hy(\theta)$ is a function of $2^m$ labels, a map with one unresolved subset is
flagged as unresolved. The frozen IEEE-39 maps carry 1.5\% such points
(Section~\ref{sec:ieee39}).

\section{Minimal Incompatibility Hypergraph}\label{sec:hypergraph}

\begin{definition}\label{def:hyper}
For fixed $\theta$ with a transversely stable base, let
$\mathcal{U}(\theta)=\{S\subseteq V:\aperp(S,\theta)\ge 0\}$. The \emph{minimal
incompatibility hypergraph} $\Hy(\theta)$ is the family of inclusion-minimal
elements of $\mathcal{U}(\theta)$, and $\kappa(\theta)=\min_{e\in\Hy}|e|$
($\kappa=\infty$ if $\Hy=\emptyset$).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Structure]\label{prop:structure}
$\Hy(\theta)$ is an antichain. A portfolio containing no hyperedge is
transversely stable. The converse is false: $\mathcal{U}$ need not be upward
closed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Minimal elements of a family form an antichain. If $S$ is unstable, a minimal
unstable subset of $S$ is a hyperedge contained in $S$. Suite S2 and the IEEE-39
census give stable supersets of unstable sets.
\end{proof}

For a target $T$, consider three planning predicates. \textbf{A}: $T$ is
transversely stable. \textbf{B}: some one-at-a-time chain
$\emptyset=S_0\subset\dots\subset S_{|T|}=T$ has every $S_j$ stable.
\textbf{C}: every $R\subseteq T$ is stable, so every implementation order
passes only through stable stationary states.

\begin{proposition}[Planning hierarchy]\label{prop:hierarchy}
C $\Rightarrow$ B $\Rightarrow$ A, and neither converse holds in general. With a
stable base, $T$ satisfies C if and only if $T$ contains no hyperedge of
$\Hy(\theta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
C gives any chain; B ends at $T$. $T$ violates C iff some $R\subseteq T$ is
unstable, iff (Proposition~\ref{prop:structure}) some hyperedge lies in $T$.
Suite S2 exhibits both converse failures.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Examples]
For A without B, take $m=2$ and diagonal vertices $A_\emptyset=-I$,
$A_{\{1\}}=\mathrm{diag}(2,-3)$, $A_{\{2\}}=\mathrm{diag}(-3,2)$, and
$A_{\{1,2\}}=-2I$. The target $\{1,2\}$ is stable, but both one-at-a-time
chains pass through an unstable singleton. For B without C, it suffices that
one proper subset is unstable while another chain avoids it. On the IEEE-39
census, each order-dependent target has exactly one unstable subset
(Section~\ref{sec:planning}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Boundary localization]\label{thm:localization}
Let $\theta(\tau)$, $\tau\in[0,1]$, be continuous with $g_z$ nonsingular along
the path for every portfolio, and let Assumption~\ref{ass:sym} hold. Then
$\Hy(\theta(\tau))$ is constant on every interval on which no $\Aperp(S,\theta(\tau))$
has an eigenvalue on the imaginary axis. Every change of $\Hy$ is bracketed by
such a crossing, at finite frequency or at $s=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\Aperp(S,\theta(\tau))$ is continuous in $\tau$ because $g_z^{-1}$ is. Its
eigenvalues are continuous, so the sign of $\aperp(S,\cdot)$ can change only
through $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda=0$. $\Hy$ is a function of the $2^m$ labels.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone class]\label{thm:monotone}
If there is an invertible $T$ such that every $B(S)=T^{-1}A(S)T$ is Metzler and
$S\subseteq S'$ implies $B(S)\le B(S')$ entrywise, then $\aperp$ is monotone on the
lattice. Stability is then hereditary, $\Hy$ consists of the minimal non-faces
of the stable family, and A $=$ B $=$ C.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For Metzler $B$ and $c\ge\max|B_{ii}|$, $\alpha(B)=\rho(B+cI)-c$; the spectral
radius is monotone on nonnegative matrices \cite{farina2000positive}, and
$\alpha$ is similarity invariant.
\end{proof}
A single robust pair $S\subset S'$ with $\aperp(S)>0>\aperp(S')$ excludes every
such realization. IEEE-39 contains three such pairs (Section~\ref{sec:planning}).

\begin{proposition}[Complexity, credited]\label{prop:complexity}
Deciding whether some $|S|\le k$ has $\alpha(A(\delta_S))\ge 0$ is NP-complete,
already for $A_G(\delta)=D_\delta W D_\delta-(k-1)I$ with $W$ a graph adjacency
matrix and $D_\delta=\mathrm{diag}(\delta)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\alpha(A_G(\delta_S))\ge 0$ with $|S|\le k$ iff $\lambda_{\max}(W[S])\ge k-1$,
iff $G[S]=K_k$ \cite{karp1972}. Membership follows from exact $LDL^\top$. This
is the clique reduction behind the NP-hardness of sparse PCA
\cite{magdon2017sparsepca}; robust stability of interval and affine families is
NP-hard as well \cite{poljak1993robust, nemirovskii1993robust}. Structured
radii under continuous perturbations are treated in \cite{katewa2020sparse}.
\end{proof}

\section{Network-Closure Factorization and Ports}\label{sec:port}

\subsection{Descriptor realization affine in the indicators}
Under matched dispatch, every candidate bus can carry \emph{both} devices: the
SG with weight $(1-\delta_i)$ and the GFL with weight $\delta_i$. An absent
device is replaced by a stable filler $\dot x=-(x-x_0)$ of the same dimension,
at its intensive equilibrium, which is common to all vertices.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:affine}
On this common realization the descriptor Jacobian is exactly affine,
$J(\delta)=J_0+\sum_i\delta_iJ_i$. Each $J_i$ is supported on the states of the
devices at bus $i$ and on the two voltage channels of bus $i$.
\end{lemma}
The weights multiply injections only, so the proof is immediate. The spectrum
of each vertex equals that of the actual portfolio plus the fillers' $-1$
eigenvalues. The reduced $A(\delta)$ is \emph{not} affine, because $g_z$
depends on $\delta$.

\subsection{Factorization}
Keep the network variables and eliminate only the device states:
$T_S(s)=g_z+g_x(sI-f_x)^{-1}f_z$ evaluated at $\delta_S$. Let $E$ select the
$2m$ voltage channels of the candidate buses, let
$\Delta Y_i(s)=E_i^\top(T_{\{i\}}-T_0)E_i$ ($2\times 2$),
$D=\blkdiag(\Delta Y_i)$, and $K=E^\top T_0^{-1}E$. Split
$K=K_d+K_o$ into its $2\times2$ diagonal blocks and the off-diagonal blocks,
which carry the network coupling between candidate buses. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
M=DK,\qquad Q=(I+DK_d)^{-1}DK_o .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Network-closure factorization]\label{thm:factorization}
Let $s$ be neither a device pole nor a zero of $\det T_0$. Then, for every
portfolio $S$,
\begin{align}
\det P_S(s)&=h_S(s)\,\det T_0(s)\,\det\!\big(I+M_{SS}(s)\big),\label{eq:fact1}\\
\det\!\big(I+M_{SS}\big)&=\prod_{i\in S}\det(I+M_{ii})\;\det\!\big(I+Q_{SS}\big),
\label{eq:fact2}
\end{align}
where $P_S$ is the descriptor pencil and $h_S(s)=\det(sI-f_x)$ is a product of
per-device factors. Moreover
$\det(I+Q_{SS})=\sum_{T\subseteq S}\mu_T$ with $\mu_\emptyset=1$, $\mu_{\{i\}}=0$,
and, for $|T|\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
\mu_T=\sum_{U:\ \mathrm{blocks}(U)=T}\det Q_{UU},
\end{equation}
the sum of principal minors of $Q$ that touch exactly the blocks of $T$.
$\mu_T$ is the M\"obius coefficient of $S\mapsto\det(I+Q_{SS})$ and is
invariant under block-diagonal changes of port basis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Schur complement of the pencil gives $\det P_S=h_S\det T_S$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}, device blocks couple only through their own bus, so
$T_S=T_0+\sum_{i\in S}E_i\Delta Y_iE_i^\top$ exactly. Sylvester's identity then
gives \eqref{eq:fact1}. Since $I+DK_d$ is block diagonal,
$I+M_{SS}=(I+DK_d)_{SS}(I+Q_{SS})$, which gives \eqref{eq:fact2}. The
expansion $\det(I+X)=\sum_U\det X_{UU}$ follows from multilinearity; grouping
minors by the blocks they touch gives \eqref{eq:mu}. $Q_{ii}=0$ gives
$\mu_{\{i\}}=0$. Möbius inversion is unique \cite{rota1964mobius}. A change
$Q\mapsto S_b^{-1}QS_b$ with $S_b$ block diagonal leaves every
$\det(I+Q_{SS})$, and hence every $\mu_T$, unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Boundaries are network-closure zeros]\label{cor:closure}
Suppose no member of $S$ alone and no device has an eigenvalue at $s^*$. Then
$s^*\in\sigma(\Aperp(S))$ iff $-1\in\sigma(M_{SS}(s^*))$, iff
$\det(I+Q_{SS}(s^*))=0$. If the network coupling is removed ($K_o=0$), no
such $s^*$ exists.
\end{corollary}

Thus every non-additive effect of a replacement portfolio is an interaction
term $\mu_T$ generated by network closure between the replaced buses. The
algebraic degree of $S\mapsto\det(I+Q_{SS})$ and $\kappa$ are different
quantities: an affine family can have $\kappa=m$, and a full-degree family can
have $\kappa=1$.

\begin{proposition}[Boundary sensitivity]\label{prop:gradient}
Let $-1$ be a simple eigenvalue of $M_{SS}(s^*,\theta^*)$ with right and left
eigenvectors $q,p$. Then the crossing eigenvalue moves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grad}
\frac{\partial s^*}{\partial\theta}=
-\frac{p^{H}\,\partial_\theta M_{SS}\,q}{p^{H}\,\partial_s M_{SS}\,q},
\end{equation}
and $\nabla_\theta\,\mathrm{Re}\,s^*$ is the normal of the boundary.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Simple-eigenvalue perturbation gives
$d\lambda=p^{H}dM\,q/p^{H}q$. The implicit function theorem applied to
$\lambda(s,\theta)=-1$ then yields \eqref{eq:grad}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Symmetry-deflated zero-frequency port}
By \eqref{eq:detid}, $\det T_S(0)$ vanishes structurally for every $S$, so the
port test is blind to aperiodic crossings. Let $U=R_x/\|R_x\|$ and
$\beta,\beta_2>0$. Relocate
$f_x^{\#}=f_x-\beta UU^\top$. The vector $x_0=w+aR_x$ with
$a=\omega_B/\beta-U^\top w/\|R_x\|$ is then a null vector of the relocated
reduced matrix. Set $f_x^{\#\#}=f_x^{\#}-\beta_2x_0x_0^\top/\|x_0\|^2$ and
$T^{\#\#}(0)=g_z-g_x(f_x^{\#\#})^{-1}f_z$.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-frequency port closure]\label{thm:port}
$\sigma(A^{\#\#})=\sigma(\Aperp)\cup\{-\beta,-\beta_2\}$ and
$\det T^{\#\#}(0)=\det g_z\det A^{\#\#}/\det f_x^{\#\#}$. Along a path on which
$\mathrm{sign}\det g_z$ and $\mathrm{sign}\det f_x^{\#\#}$ are constant,
$\mathrm{sign}\det T^{\#\#}(0)$ changes iff the number of positive real
eigenvalues of $\Aperp$ changes parity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Brauer's rank-one theorem \cite{brauer1952} moves one zero to $-\beta$. The
Jordan partner becomes the exact null vector $x_0$, which a second rank-one
update moves to $-\beta_2$. The determinant identity is the Schur formula.
Complex pairs contribute $|\lambda|^2>0$, so
$\mathrm{sign}\det\Aperp=(-1)^{\dim\Aperp}(-1)^{\#\{\lambda>0\}}$.
\end{proof}
The test does not see complex crossings; those are the domain of
\eqref{eq:fact1}. It also does not see coalescences of two positive real
eigenvalues, where nothing crosses $s=0$.

\subsection{Deviations from the preregistration}
Every deviation is listed here and in Table~\ref{tab:mc}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The P1 spectral-identity rule matched the numerically split Jordan pair.
It was amended after a one-draw smoke test and before the run, with the
threshold unchanged.
\item The P2 rule failed as implemented, because the bisection stopped at the

left the algebraic chart.
\end{enumerate}
In the second-order audit, the first run used a first-order-hold coefficient
that was $1/\Delta t$ too large. The error was found before any interpretation
and corrected; only the corrected run is reported.

\section{IEEE 39-Bus Results}\label{sec:ieee39}

\subsection{Benchmark}
The IEEE 39-bus system \cite{athay1979} is taken from the ANDES dynamic case
\cite{cui2021andes, andes_ieee39_dynamic_case}, with ten two-axis SGs; bus~39
represents the interconnection and is never replaced. The documented IEEEX1
exciter is harmonized into a first-order AVR (gain of the regulator, time
constant of the exciter), a declared model change. The core
candidates are SGs 30, 33, 35, 37. Replacing all four displaces
2096.6~MW of active dispatch on 4270.7~MVA of converter rating. Every result in
this section is conditional on this model.

\subsection{Policy dependence of the witness structure}
Three policy planes were mapped: F7A ($g\times k$), F7B ($g\times t$), and F7C
($g\times h$). All 345\,229 points were re-audited in transverse coordinates on
the direct path. No resolved label changed relative to the frozen maps; 1.5\%
of points remain within the classifier's numerical resolution. The planes carry
30, 36, and 16 distinct hypergraphs, with up to 15 hypergraphs behind a single
value of $\kappa$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}).

At the reference point P4 ($g=0.03625$, $k=1.425$, $t=1.5$, $h=1$),
$\Hy=\{\{30,33,35,37\}\}$ and $\aperp=+0.127$~s$^{-1}$: every proper subset
is stable and the four-unit portfolio is not. At full voltage-support gain
(P$_\infty$: $g=1$, $k=0.5$), $\Hy=\emptyset$. On the pure-policy line
$t=0.852$ the minimal witness contracts $4\to3\to2$ and re-expands $3\to4\to\emptyset$
as $g$ increases.

The failing mode is the 0.64--0.71~Hz inter-area family, not the amount of
dispatch removed (Fig.~\ref{fig:dispatch}). Among the 512-portfolio census,
consider the 25 stable four-unit portfolios whose displaced active dispatch
matches that of the 10 unstable ones. In all 25 the family stays damped
($\max\mathrm{Re}\le-0.114$~s$^{-1}$, $\zeta\ge3.9\%$). It is unstable in 8 of
the 10 failing portfolios; the other two fail through a different mode. Total
displaced dispatch predicts the minimal coalition poorly (AUC 0.57).

\subsection{Network-closure anatomy of the boundaries}
Ten frozen boundaries were re-located and analyzed with
Theorem~\ref{thm:factorization}: the nine witness changes of the F7B line
$t=0.852$ and the flagship boundary on the P4 line ($g^*=0.2077$, 0.706~Hz).
\begin{itemize}
\item The descriptor blocks $f_x,f_z,g_x,g_z$ are affine in $\delta$ to
$4.5\times10^{-16}$ (order-$\ge2$ M\"obius coefficients). Each replacement
touches only its own devices and bus (a $2\times2$ $g_z$ block).
\item \eqref{eq:fact1} holds to $1.4\times10^{-13}$ for all 16 subsets at all
ten points, and the critical eigenvalue of $M_{SS}$ equals $-1$ to
$8.5\times10^{-14}$.
\item The local factor $\prod_i\det(I+M_{ii})$ stays between 0.08 and 0.31.
The zero is carried entirely by $\det(I+Q_{SS})$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:closure}).
\item No truncation of \eqref{eq:mu} below order $|S|$ vanishes: the residual
is 0.29--0.40 for triples at order 2, and 0.59--0.60 (order 2) and 0.13--0.16
(order 3) for the four-unit portfolio.
\end{itemize}
Every observed boundary is therefore closed only by the full-order network
interaction of the coalition whose label changes, which is the mechanism behind
$\kappa$. Table~\ref{tab:anatomy} lists the ten events and Fig.~\ref{fig:path}
places them on the F7B line.

Table~\ref{tab:orders} gives the build-up at the flagship boundary, order by
order. The interaction coefficients of orders 2, 3, and 4 have comparable
magnitude (0.51, 0.45, 0.16). The fourth-order term, a sum of principal minors
of $Q$ touching all four replaced buses, is exactly what closes the
determinant. On 120 random $(\theta,s)$ draws (suite P3), the full-order
coefficient of $\det(I+M_{SS})$ ranges from $7.8\times10^{-4}$ to 469 and never
vanishes. No lower-degree representation of the portfolio family is exact on
this benchmark.

magnitude to $4.3\times10^{-6}$ (relative), and the frequency drift to

\begin{itemize}

For finite disturbances we used three frozen families: a bus-16 load pulse
(D1), an SG36 mechanical-power dip (D2), and a GFL output dip (D3). We also
declared model-scope guards: GFL terminal voltage in $[0.9,1.1]$~pu, GFL
current $\le1.2$~pu, PLL and COI frequency bands, and the documented exciter and
stabilizer limits. Findings:
\begin{itemize}
\item Inside these guards, the finite-disturbance hypergraph equals
$\Hy(\theta)$ for all six (point, family) combinations.
\item No transversely stable portfolio fails inside the guards: 84 of 96
thresholds are guard activations (GFL voltage band, stabilizer limit).
\item Normal-form analysis on the exact transverse system shows that the three
examined oscillatory boundaries are subcritical Hopf points
\cite{kuznetsov2004bifurcation} (Table~\ref{tab:hopf}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Planning consequences}\label{sec:planning}
Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy} turns $\Hy$ into constraints. On the 512
portfolios of nine candidates (30--38) at one reference policy, 327 are
transversely stable, 324 are safe in every order, and three are final-stable
but order-dependent. In each of the three, the only unstable subset is the
four-set without unit 34, so exactly the orders that convert 34 last are
unsafe. These three pairs also exclude the monotone class
(Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone}).

The MW-optimal plan (31+32+34+35+37+38, 3652.3~MW / 6499.9~MVA) is safe in any
order. At P4 the optimal any-order-safe core plan is 30+33+35 (1775.1~MW); at
P$_\infty$ it is the full core (2096.6~MW).

A condenser with a damped swing mode restores P4 above 2.48\% of the retired
rating (106.0~MVA; TDS threshold 2.47\%), while an undamped low-inertia
condenser is itself unstable. Keeping a 200-MW disturbance inside the declared
converter voltage band requires 684--792~MVA. That is an envelope requirement
of this model, not a stability requirement. Table~\ref{tab:planning}
summarizes the three planning problems, preregistered before any threshold was
computed:
\begin{itemize}
\item P1 maximizes displaced active dispatch subject to containing no
hyperedge.
\item P2 adds the nonlinear hyperedges at preregistered severities (200~MW for
D1 and D2, 0.35 for D3).
\item P3 minimizes the damped-condenser rating.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to existing methods}
Table~\ref{tab:methods} places the object among established method families.
The hypergraph does not replace any of them. Its per-portfolio labels
\emph{are} generalized Nyquist tests. What it adds is the organization of those
labels over binary replacement decisions and their dependence on the converter
policy, which none of the configuration-level criteria resolves.
Flagship-only Nyquist, single-port impedance, and short-circuit-ratio screens
miss the coalition changes on IEEE-39. Modal-sensitivity, additive, and pairwise
predictors of the four-unit instability were correct in 22, 30, and 38 of 52
cases, respectively.

\subsection{Use in retirement planning}
Three uses follow directly.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The any-order-safe portfolios are exactly those avoiding every hyperedge
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hierarchy}). This is a set-packing constraint that a
unit-commitment or capacity-expansion model can carry.
\item Order dependence is real but rare on IEEE-39 (three of 327 stable
targets at one policy, 131 of 30\,755 on random paths). Where it occurs, the
unsafe orders are identified exactly.
\item The policy is a planning variable. At fixed network and dispatch, it
moves $\kappa$ between 1 and $\infty$, and the boundary normals given by
\eqref{eq:grad} are exact local directions for retuning.
\end{enumerate}
Evaluating $2^m$ labels is exponential in general
(Proposition~\ref{prop:complexity}); with $m=4$ it takes 16 evaluations. The
factorization in \eqref{eq:fact1} evaluates all portfolios from one base
network and $m$ local increments at a common operating point.

\section{Corroboration and Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\emph{Kundur two-area system.} Policy dependence recurs: 61/61 and 36/36
policy lines change $\Hy$, which is never empty. All 194 aperiodic
(Q/V-integrator) crossings are real transverse crossings. They motivated
Theorem~\ref{thm:port}; its preregistered holdout (Fig.~\ref{fig:port}) is the
physical validation, whereas the 194 design crossings are not counted.

\emph{IEEE 68-bus system} \cite{canizares2015benchmark}. The documented model is
reproduced (15/15 modes to $5\times10^{-4}$~Hz), but the preregistered
four-candidate policy map is empty. Policy dependence appears only at $\ge 6$
of 12 replaced plants ($\kappa=6\to9\to11$). The structure is therefore
system dependent, not universal.

\emph{Independent implementation.} ANDES reproduces the power flow, the base
inter-area mode (within 4.1\%), and the governor damping direction. ANDES does
not implement the converter model used here, so no portfolio result is claimed
as independently reproduced.

\emph{Model scope.} The model is a phasor DAE, not an electromagnetic
transient model. It has no converter current limits, ride-through logic, or DC
link. The frozen models have no primary frequency restoration, so all stability
statements are transverse. No certificate is claimed: sampled small-gain and
principal-minor screens give $\rho=2.9$--$12.3>1$, and a local Lyapunov bound
reaches at most $10^{-11}$ of the measured threshold.

\section{Conclusion}
The minimal incompatibility hypergraph is a planning object for SG retirement:
it is exactly defined in transverse coordinates, it changes only at
imaginary-axis crossings, and it characterizes the portfolios that are safe in
every implementation order. Its non-additivity is a principal-minor expansion
of the network closure between replaced buses. On IEEE-39 every observed
boundary is closed only by the full-order interaction of the changing
coalition, and the converter reactive policy changes which coalitions fail.
Every statement was tested by preregistered Monte Carlo suites, synthetic and
physical. All passed except one IEEE-39 localization rule, which failed as
implemented; that failure is reported together with its post-hoc correction. The four-unit coalition is conditional on the absence of primary
frequency control. Nonlinear composability adds no stricter structure within
model scope, and the structure is system dependent. Extending the analysis to
limiter-equipped, governed, and mixed grid-forming benchmarks requires new,
separately versioned models.

\section{Proof Details}\label{app:proofs}
\emph{Nonlinear drift in Theorem~\ref{thm:transverse}.} Fix $x$ in the chart
domain and let $z=\psi(x)$. Consider $x_c=x+c\,w$. Speeds enter the reduced
equations only through $\dot\delta=\omega_B(\omega-1)$ and, for a GFL, through
$\dot\vartheta=k_{p,\rm pll}v_q+x_{\rm pll}$. In both cases the shift adds the
constant $c\,\omega_B$ to the angle derivatives, and nothing else. The power-form
swing equation with $D=0$ does not contain $\omega$ on its right-hand side,
the stabilizer uses electrical power, and the network is frequency
independent. Hence $\psi(x_c)=\psi(x)$ and $r(x_c)=r(x)+c\,\omega_BR_x$. The
rotation identity follows from $\psi(x+\phi R_x)=e^{j\phi}\psi(x)$ and the
invariance of $f$ under a common rotation of angles and voltages. Both
identities hold for every $x$, so they are properties of the vector field, not
only of the linearization.

\emph{Locality in Theorem~\ref{thm:factorization}.} Order the device states by
bus. On the common realization, $f_x$ is block diagonal by device; $f_z$ and
$g_x$ couple a device only to the two channels of its own bus; and the device
contribution to $g_z$ is a $2\times2$ block on that bus. Therefore
$g_x(sI-f_x)^{-1}f_z=\sum_{\rm devices}E_b\,G_b(s)\,E_b^\top$, where $G_b$ is the
device's $2\times2$ admittance. With the network and load terms common to all
vertices, $T_S-T_0=\sum_{i\in S}E_i\Delta Y_iE_i^\top$. The increment
$\Delta Y_i$ does not depend on $S$ because every device stays at its common
intensive equilibrium (matched dispatch). The residual of this locality on the
direct (non-common) path is bounded by the power-flow tolerance and was measured
below $8\times10^{-8}$.

\emph{Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}.} If member $i$ alone has no eigenvalue at
$s^*$, then $\det(I+M_{ii})=\det T_{\{i\}}(s^*)/\det T_0(s^*)\neq0$. The zero of
\eqref{eq:fact2} must then be a zero of $\det(I+Q_{SS})$. With $K_o=0$, $Q=0$
and $\det(I+Q_{SS})=1$.

\section{Nonlinear Simulation Definitions}\label{app:tds}
The TDS integrates the full DAE (BDF, relative tolerance $10^{-7}$, maximum
step 0.05~s). The network equations are solved by Newton at every step.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pulse.} Each disturbance is applied for 0.5~s and followed by 30~s of
free response.
\item \emph{Deviation measure.} $D(t)$ is the larger of the transverse state and
voltage deviations, normalized by $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-2}$.
\item \emph{Recovers.} $D$ decays below 10\% of its early peak with a negative
fitted envelope rate.
\item \emph{Fails.} $D$ exceeds twice the early peak with positive growth over
the last 10~s, or the relative rotor angle exceeds $\pi$.
\item \emph{Outside model scope.} A guard trips: bus voltage outside
$[0.8,1.2]$, GFL terminal voltage outside $[0.9,1.1]$, GFL current or apparent
power above 1.2, reactive power above 1.0, PLL or speed frequency outside
$[-3,1.8]$~Hz, COI frequency beyond $\pm0.5$~Hz, field voltage outside the
documented exciter limits, or stabilizer output beyond 0.1 (all per unit on
the device base).
\end{itemize}
Thresholds $r_S$ are obtained by continuation on a frozen amplitude grid
followed by bisection. A threshold whose first event is a guard is
scope-censored, and the minimum of the censored thresholds defines
$\rho_{\rm scope}$.
