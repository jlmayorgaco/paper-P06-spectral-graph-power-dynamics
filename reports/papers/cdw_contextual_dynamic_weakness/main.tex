\begin{abstract}
Weak-bus and grid-strength indices rank locations once, for a given network.
When synchronous generators (SGs) are replaced by grid-following converters
(GFLs) unit by unit, the quantity a planner needs is the marginal small-signal
effect of the next replacement, and this effect depends on which units have
already been replaced. On the IEEE 39-bus system with a documented GFL model, we
evaluate all $2^9$ replacement portfolios at 63 controller policies, 24 of them a
new preregistered holdout, and measure the change of the transverse spectral
abscissa $\aperp$ caused by each replacement in each portfolio. In \cntCNew{}
base-stable new-holdout policies the same replacement moves the same tracked
electromechanical mode left in one stable portfolio and right in a portfolio
that contains it. By the chain identity for set-function marginals, each such
nested reversal is certified by a discrete-curvature term of the required sign,
so $\aperp$ is neither submodular nor supermodular. An oracle fixed ranking of
units orders \pstarFULLNew{} of resolved pairs and is materially wrong in
\regretFULLNew{} of next-replacement decisions, a failure carried mostly by
transitions into fast converter-control modes; restricted to electromechanical
transitions it is correct for \pstarEMNew{} of pairs. For branch reinforcement,
the portfolio-conditioned re-equilibrated eigenvalue sensitivity ranks finite
reinforcements with median Spearman \gbpreregmedianrhoDtotal{} on
preregistration-eligible held-out conditions (\gbprimarymedianrhoDtotal{} over
all 64), against \gbpreregmedianrhoSone{} for the discovery-selected static index and
\gbprimarymedianrhooraclestatic{} for the best of nine static indices chosen per
condition. A frozen WECC library GFL chain reproduces the ranking method but not
the reversals or the ranking itself. Replacement planning should therefore rank
interventions in the portfolio context in which they are applied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{G}{rid-strength} indices summarize how well a network supports
converter-interfaced resources at a location. The short-circuit ratio (SCR) and
its multi-infeed generalizations---the site-dependent SCR~\cite{wu2018sdscr},
the generalized SCR~\cite{xin2016gscr,dong2019gscr,zhou2023heterogeneous},
the generalized operational SCR~\cite{liu2024goscr} and small-signal strength
metrics for all-inverter systems~\cite{ma2024strength}---and impedance-based
metrics~\cite{henderson2024gsim,lamrani2025fagim} are used to flag weak buses
and to rank where converters or reinforcements should go. Modal participation
analysis~\cite{ebrahimzadeh2018busPF,zhu2022greybox} and impedance-based
eigenvalue sensitivities~\cite{zhu2023rootcause} play the same role for
dynamic criticality.

These tools answer a question about a fixed system. Retirement of synchronous
generators poses a different question. Units are replaced one at a time, and the
planner must decide which unit to replace next \emph{given} the units already
replaced, and which branch to reinforce \emph{given} the resulting portfolio.
The object of interest is the contextual marginal
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marg}
\Delta_i\aperp(S)=\aperp(S\cup\{i\})-\aperp(S),
\end{equation}
the change of the rightmost transverse eigenvalue's real part when unit $i$ is
replaced in portfolio $S$. If $\Delta_i\aperp(S)$ kept its sign and its order
across portfolios, a fixed ranking of units (a weak-bus list) would be adequate
for sequencing. We show on the IEEE 39-bus system that it does not, and we
characterize where the failure comes from.

Several ingredients are known and are credited here rather than claimed. The
effect of a branch reinforcement on linear stability can have either sign
(Braess-type behavior in oscillator networks~\cite{coletta2016braess} and in
flows~\cite{schafer2022braess}; operating-point-dependent edge weights
in~\cite{song2018network}). The effect of adding a unit type can depend on the
existing mix~\cite{li2023intrinsic,markovic2021lowinertia,stanojev2023vim}.
Eigenvalue sensitivities that account for the re-solved operating point are
classical~\cite{smed1993feasible,nam2000eigsens} and have been applied to
redispatch~\cite{mendozaarmenta2016redispatch}, operational
parameters~\cite{li2019opsens} and, in a recent preprint, line
admittances~\cite{joswigjones2026sens}. Transmission switching changes
small-signal margins~\cite{saric2015rapid,li2018switching}. Submodular
structure is standard in actuator placement~\cite{summers2016submodularity},
and its failure for natural dynamic set functions is known~\cite{olshevsky2018nonsupermodularity}.

Within a focused 2020--2026 search (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}) we found no
study that (i) certifies sign reversals of the same SG$\to$GFL replacement across
\emph{nested} stable portfolios on the \emph{same tracked electromechanical
mode}, (ii) measures how often an oracle-optimal fixed ranking selects a
materially worse next replacement, and (iii) validates a portfolio-conditioned
re-equilibrated branch sensitivity as a \emph{ranking} of finite reinforcements
against static indicators on held-out conditions. The contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item[C1)] \emph{Nested contextual sign reversal.} The same replacement moves the
same electromechanical mode in opposite directions in two nested stable
portfolios, in \cntCNew{} base-stable policies of a new preregistered holdout.
The classical chain identity certifies each reversal by a discrete-curvature
term, so $\aperp$ is neither submodular nor supermodular.
\item[C2)] \emph{Fixed-ranking insufficiency and its modal anatomy.} The best
fixed ranking of units is materially wrong in \regretFULLNew{} of stable
next-replacement decisions and does not transfer across policies. The failure is
carried by transitions into fast converter-control modes; restricted to
electromechanical transitions a fixed ranking orders \pstarEMNew{} of pairs.
\item[C3)] \emph{Portfolio-conditioned reinforcement ranking.} The
re-equilibrated first-order sensitivity of $\aperp$ to branch admittance ranks
finite reinforcements far better than nine static indicators on held-out
policies and parameter draws, and the re-equilibration term, zero for controller
coordinates, is what separates it from the frozen derivative.
\item[C4)] \emph{Electromechanical topology effects.} Single admissible branch
actions both remove and create electromechanical incompatibilities, and seven
static topology scores fail a preregistered prediction rule.
\end{itemize}

A cross-model holdout with a frozen WECC library GFL chain delimits these
claims: the ranking method transfers, the reversals and the ranking itself do
not. All thresholds, holdout points and tests were frozen before the
computations reported here; deviations are logged. The study is phasor-domain
small-signal analysis on one benchmark; no electromagnetic-transient claim is
made.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Model}
The network is the IEEE 39-bus system~\cite{athay1979} (46 branches, ten SGs,
bus 39 an interconnection equivalent) in phasor form, with constant-power loads.
The system is the index-one DAE $\dot x=f(x,z;p)$, $0=g(x,z;p)$, where $x$
collects device states, $z$ the rectangular bus voltages and $p$ parameters.
At an equilibrium $(x^\star,z^\star)$ the reduced Jacobian is
$A=f_x-f_zg_z^{-1}g_x$.

Each SG uses a two-axis model on its own base~\cite{sauer1998} with $D=0$,
constant mechanical power, a first-order AVR
$T_E\dot e_{fd}=K_A(v_{\rm ref}+v_s-|V|)-e_{fd}$ and a power-input stabilizer.
Each GFL has a synchronous-reference-frame PLL, filtered power measurements, PI
outer and inner loops behind a filter reactance, and a leaky Q/V regulator
$q_{\rm cmd}=k_{p,v}\,g\,e+x_v$, $\dot x_v=k_{i,v}\,g\,e-w(x_v-q_{\rm ref})$,
$e=v_{\rm ref}-|V|$, $w=0.05$~rad/s. Parameters are those of the harmonized
benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:param}. Converter current limits, DC links and
modulation delays are not modeled.

A \emph{policy} $\theta=(g,k,t,h)$ sets the Q/V gain $g$ of every GFL, a common
scale $k$ of the surviving SGs' AVR gains, a scale $t$ of their AVR time
constants, and a heterogeneity exponent $h$ with $T_{E,i}\mapsto G(T_{E,i}/G)^h$,
$G$ the geometric mean. Replacing unit $i$ installs a GFL with the same active
and reactive dispatch and the SG rating (matched dispatch), so the AC power flow
is the same for every portfolio.

\subsection{Transverse stability}
With $D=0$ and no governors the models have an exact rotation symmetry and a
neutral frequency mode, which produce two structural zero eigenvalues. We
evaluate stability on the transverse quotient that removes them and write
$\aperp$ for the largest real part of the transverse spectrum. A portfolio is
STABLE if all transverse eigenvalues satisfy ${\rm Re}\,\lambda\le-2\times10^{-3}$
and UNSTABLE if one has ${\rm Re}\,\lambda\ge2\times10^{-3}$; cases in the band
between are unresolved and are never counted as stable.

\subsection{Contextual marginals and modal levels}
For a policy $\theta$ and candidate set $V$, the set function is
$f(S)=\aperp(S;\theta)$, $S\subseteq V$. A marginal \eqref{eq:marg} is
\emph{material} if $|\Delta_i f(S)|\ge\tau_{\rm mat}=0.01$~s$^{-1}$, about
0.2~\% damping ratio at 0.7~Hz and 8~\% of the flagship margin; its sign class is
$-1$ (stabilizing), $0$ or $+1$ (destabilizing). A context $S$ is admissible if
$S$ is STABLE.

A change of $\aperp$ can be a jump of the rightmost eigenvalue from one mode to
another, including fast converter-control modes. We therefore grade each
marginal $(S,i)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[A)] $S$ is STABLE (global $\aperp$);
\item[B)] in addition, the critical mode of $S$ has a match in $S\cup\{i\}$
(bus-voltage shape MAC $\ge0.8$, $|\Delta f|\le0.15$~Hz) that is the critical mode
of $S\cup\{i\}$;
\item[C)] in addition, both critical frequencies lie in the electromechanical
(EM) band 0.1--2.0~Hz;
\item[D)] (for pairs of contexts) C holds and the two contexts' critical modes
match each other.
\end{itemize}
Under level~C, $\Delta_i f(S)$ is the displacement of one tracked EM mode that
remains rightmost.

\section{Why Node Weakness Is Not Intrinsic}\label{sec:theory}

For $S\subseteq V$ and distinct $i,j\notin S$ let
$d_{ij}(S)=f(S\cup\{i,j\})-f(S\cup\{i\})-f(S\cup\{j\})+f(S)$, the one-step
second difference. Directly, $d_{ij}(S)=\Delta_if(S\cup\{j\})-\Delta_if(S)$.
Recall that $f$ is submodular if $\Delta_if(S)\ge\Delta_if(T)$ for $S\subseteq
T$, $i\notin T$, equivalently if $d_{ij}(S)\le0$ everywhere, and supermodular
with the inequalities
reversed~\cite{topkis1978,lovasz1983,fujishige2005,bach2013}. None of the
following algebra is new; it is used to turn observed reversals into
certificates.

\begin{lemma}[chain identity]\label{lem:chain}
Let $S_0\subseteq V$, $j_1,\dots,j_m\in V\setminus S_0$ distinct,
$S_r=S_{r-1}\cup\{j_r\}$, and $i\notin S_m$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
\Delta_if(S_m)=\Delta_if(S_0)+\sum_{r=1}^m d_{ij_r}(S_{r-1}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{IEEEproof}
$d_{ij_r}(S_{r-1})=\Delta_if(S_r)-\Delta_if(S_{r-1})$; sum over $r$.
\end{IEEEproof}

A \emph{nested reversal} of $i$ is a pair $S_1\subsetneq S_2$, $i\notin S_2$, with
$\Delta_if(S_1)$ and $\Delta_if(S_2)$ in opposite sign classes. Its direction is
stabilizing$\to$destabilizing (s$\to$d) if the negative marginal is at $S_1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:abc}
(a) If $f$ is submodular, no nested reversal of direction s$\to$d exists.
(b) If $f$ is supermodular, none of direction d$\to$s exists. (c) If nested
reversals of both directions occur, $f$ is neither submodular nor
supermodular.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Under submodularity every term of \eqref{eq:chain} is nonpositive, so the
marginal cannot increase along a growing chain; (b) is symmetric and (c) is the
contrapositive.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{proposition}[witness]\label{prop:witness}
Let $D=\Delta_if(S_2)-\Delta_if(S_1)$ and $m=|S_2\setminus S_1|$. Along every
chain from $S_1$ to $S_2$ some term satisfies $d_{ij_r}(S_{r-1})\ge D/m$ if $D>0$
($\le D/m$ if $D<0$). A material reversal has $|D|\ge2\tau_{\rm mat}$, so the
witness has $|d|\ge2\tau_{\rm mat}/m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
The $m$ terms of \eqref{eq:chain} sum to $D$; the largest is at least the mean.
\end{IEEEproof}

Nestedness matters. On $V=\{i,a,b\}$ take $f(\emptyset)=f(a)=f(b)=f(ab)=0$,
$f(i)=2$, $f(ai)=-1$, $f(bi)=1$, $f(abi)=-2$: all six second differences are
$\le0$, so $f$ is submodular, yet $\Delta_if(\{a\})<0<\Delta_if(\{b\})$. A reversal
between incomparable contexts is therefore compatible with submodularity; a
nested one is not (Proposition~\ref{prop:abc}).

\begin{proposition}[preference drift]\label{prop:drift}
For a chain as in Lemma~\ref{lem:chain} and $i,j\notin S_m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift}
[\Delta_if-\Delta_jf]_{S_0}^{S_m}=\sum_{r=1}^m\big(d_{ij_r}-d_{jj_r}\big)(S_{r-1}).
\end{equation}
If $\Delta_if(S_0)\le\Delta_jf(S_0)-\tau$ and $\Delta_jf(S_m)\le\Delta_if(S_m)-\tau$,
every fixed ranking of $V$ errs by at least $\tau$ in one of the two contexts, and
some term of \eqref{eq:drift} is at least $2\tau/m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
Apply \eqref{eq:chain} to $i$ and $j$ and subtract; a fixed ranking places $i$
before $j$ or after it in both contexts.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:heter}
If $f(S)=h(|S|)+\sum_{k\in S}w_k$ then $d_{ik}=d_{jk}$ and the ranking by $w$ is
exact in every context, whether $h$ is concave, convex or neither. What defeats
a fixed ranking is the heterogeneity of curvature across units, not its sign.
The two are measured separately below: sign structure by nested reversals,
ranking failure by an oracle ranking.
\end{remark}

Because $\aperp$ is a maximum over modes, terms of \eqref{eq:chain} on
intermediate portfolios can reflect mode switches even when the two endpoint
marginals are level~C. We record whether a certifying term exists whose four
portfolios are all stable with EM critical modes (an \emph{EM-clean witness}).

\section{Dynamic Intervention Sensitivity}\label{sec:sens}

\subsection{Frozen and total derivatives}
Let $w=(x,z,r)$ collect states, bus voltages and the references $r$ that the
dispatch semantics frees. Under \emph{scheduled-power-and-voltage} (SPR)
semantics the SG and GFL references are re-solved so that every unit keeps its
scheduled active power and terminal voltage; the equilibrium is
$R(w;a)=0$ for a parameter $a$. For a simple critical eigenvalue $\lambda$ with
right and left eigenvectors $u$, $v$ of $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tot}
\frac{d\lambda}{da}=\frac{v^{H}\big(\partial_aA+\partial_wA[\dot w]\big)u}{v^Hu},\qquad
\dot w=-R_w^{-1}R_a,
\end{equation}
by the implicit function theorem. The first term alone is the \emph{frozen}
derivative $\Dfro$; the full expression is the \emph{total} derivative $\Dtot$,
and $\Dtot-\Dfro$ is the re-equilibration term. Equation~\eqref{eq:tot} is the
feasible-sensitivity construction of~\cite{smed1993feasible,nam2000eigsens}
applied to portfolio-specific operating points; no novelty is claimed for it.

\begin{remark}
If $a$ enters only a controller of a unit whose scheduled output is fixed
(the GFL gain $g_i$, a PLL scale, an AVR gain), the SPR equilibrium does not
depend on $a$, so $\dot w=0$ and $\Dtot=\Dfro$. Network and operating-point
parameters (branch admittances, voltage setpoints, loads) move the equilibrium.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Branch interventions and baselines}
A branch intervention scales the complete two-port admittance of branch $e$ by
$\gamma_e$ (tap unchanged). The ranking score of branch $e$ in portfolio $S$ is
$-d\aperp/d\gamma_e$ from \eqref{eq:tot} at $\gamma_e=1$. The truth is the
finite, fully re-solved change $-[\aperp(\gamma_e)-\aperp(1)]$ at
$\gamma_e\in\{1.10,1.25,1.50\}$. The static baselines are: branch active and
apparent flow $|P_e|$, $|S_e|$; impedance $|z_e|$; the endpoint electrical
distance on the subtransient bus impedance matrix; effective resistance and
Fiedler edge score $(u_{2,f}-u_{2,t})^2$ on the coupling Laplacian $L_B$;
weighted edge betweenness; endpoint $dV/dQ$; and the change of the
generalized SCR of the target portfolio under $\gamma_e=1.5$. We also report
the conventional sensitivity of the base (all-SG) critical mode, and an
independent port-form derivative~\cite{zhu2023rootcause} computed from the
network return difference at the critical frequency.

\section{Experimental Design}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Policies and holdouts}
Policies lie in the domain $u\in[0,1]$ ($g=u^2$), $k\in[0.5,2.3]$,
$t\in[0.5,3.0]$, $h\in[0,2]$. The \emph{discovery} set has 15 points on
axis-aligned lines through the flagship policy P4 $=(0.03625,1.425,1.5,1)$. An
\emph{old holdout} of 24 Latin-hypercube points was used in a first campaign.
The \emph{new holdout} HARDENING\_H01--H24 is a maximin Latin hypercube (best of
2000 candidate designs by minimum pairwise distance, seed 20260929), frozen
before any computation. No point was chosen, replaced or screened for
stability. Policies whose all-SG base is unstable (\nNewUnstable{} new, \nOldUnstable{}
old) are kept and excluded only from tests that require a stable base.

\subsection{Parameter envelopes}
Four envelopes perturb machine and converter parameters within declared bounds:
fleet-wide machine groups (EM-f), unit-level machine groups (EM-u), converter
groups at every candidate (EC), and both (EMC). A fresh set of ten
Latin-hypercube draws per envelope (seed 20260930) is evaluated at P4. These are
designed conditions, so we report coverage over the declared draws, not
probabilities.

\subsection{Computations}
At every policy all 512 portfolios of $V_9$ are solved with bus-voltage mode
shapes (32\,256 portfolio evaluations over 63 policies). Branch sensitivities
are computed at 88 new-holdout conditions (24 policies for targets $V_4$ and
$V_9$; 40 draws for $V_4$) for all 46 branches with three finite steps each.
The implicit-function derivatives agree with small-step finite differences
within 5\,\% (or $10^{-4}$ absolute) for \ivLinks{} of \ivN{} branch cases.
Topology is evaluated at 16 policies for 35 admissible outages and 46 doublings.
Reruns of selected tasks were byte-identical.

\subsection{Cross-model holdout}
No validated grid-forming model exists in our code base. The one existing
validated alternative dynamic converter is a WECC library GFL chain (PLL, REGC,
REEC-B and REPC-A blocks~\cite{pourbeik2017generic} in
ANDES~\cite{cui2021andes}) with a parameter set frozen and validated against an
EMT implementation in earlier work. We attach it, unchanged and in constant-$Q$
mode, to a transcription of our machine models that reproduces the internal
$\aperp$ of the all-SG base within $2\times10^{-7}$~s$^{-1}$ at every tested
policy (ALT-WECC). It has no $g$, so policies act through the AVR coordinates.
Eight policies were fixed in advance.

\subsection{Statistics}
Holdout policies and envelope draws are designed points, so intervals are
percentile bootstraps over the tested conditions (policies, or envelopes, as
clusters; $10^4$ resamples), read as the stability of a summary across the
design. Three secondary tests form a Holm family; all gates are effect-size
thresholds fixed in the preregistration.

\subsection{Curvature certificates}
Along the canonical chain from $S_1$ to $S_2$, every one of the \newCpairs{}
new-holdout level-C reversals has a certifying curvature term satisfying the
bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} (Fig.~\ref{fig:curv}); for \newCemClean{} of
them an EM-clean witness exists. Over all one-step squares on the new holdout,
second differences exceed $+\tau_{\rm res}$ ($10^{-3}$~s$^{-1}$) and fall below
$-\tau_{\rm res}$ in comparable proportions (Fig.~\ref{fig:curv}). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:abc}(c), $\aperp$ is neither submodular nor supermodular at
every tested policy with reversals of both directions. The heavy tails of
Fig.~\ref{fig:curv} ($|d|$ up to $10^4$~s$^{-1}$) are fast-mode events, which is why
the certificates are stated on level-C endpoints.

\subsection{Fixed rankings and their modal anatomy}
For each base-stable policy, the fixed ranking of the nine units that maximizes
agreement with the policy's own resolved preferences is found exactly by
dynamic programming. This is the most favorable case for a node-only ranking.
Table~\ref{tab:ranking} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ranking} give the results. Over all
transitions, the oracle ranking orders \pstarFULLNew{} of resolved pairs on the
new holdout (interval \pstarNewLo--\pstarNewHi) and its next-replacement choice
is at least $\tau_{\rm mat}$ worse than the context-aware choice in
\regretFULLNew{} of stable contexts; \tOneK/\tOneN{} policies have $p^\star<0.90$.
The old-to-new shift is \shiftPstar{} in $p^\star$ (interval \shiftPstarLo{} to

ranking fitted at one policy and used at another is materially wrong in

The stratification changes the reading. Restricted to transitions whose
critical modes stay in the EM band, the oracle ranking orders \pstarEMNew{} of
pairs and errs materially in \regretEMNew{} of decisions; on level-C marginals
the figures are \pstarSAMENew{} and \regretSAMENew. In \regretNfast{} of the
\regretNbad{} materially wrong decisions on the new holdout (\regretFastShare), the
unit chosen by the ranking moves the rightmost eigenvalue out of the EM band,
almost always onto a fast converter-control mode. The fixed-ranking failure
therefore comes mainly from replacements that trigger such a jump in some
contexts and not in others. A fixed
ranking cannot anticipate these jumps. For EM dynamics alone it is a reasonable
approximation, and the reversals of Section~\ref{sec:results}-A are too small and
too rare to change most EM decisions.

\subsection{A negative result on modal mixing}
A first campaign reported that controller-weighted modal mixing of the network
port operator correlates with the number of reversing units ($\rho=\mixOldOrigRho$ on
the old holdout). That quantity was evaluated at each policy's own critical
frequency, which confounds controller effects with frequency movement. At a
fixed reference frequency the old-holdout correlation falls to \mixOldFixedRho. On the
new holdout the preregistered fixed-frequency test gives $\rho=\mixNewRho$
(permutation $p=\mixNewP$, Holm-adjusted \tThreePholm), which misses the
preregistered bar ($p\le0.01$). An exact two-factor decomposition attributes
\mixVarC{} of the variance of the mixing change to the controller term and the
rest to frequency movement. The mixing interpretation of contextuality is not
supported.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{Relation to strength metrics.} Grid-strength indices are properties of
the network and of a location. The results indicate that two different objects
are being asked of them: a location's support for converters, which such indices
approximate, and the ranking of the next intervention in a given portfolio,
which depends on the portfolio. The static indices we tested are not
uninformative---effective resistance puts some of the strongest branches in its
top five, and the distinction between outages and doublings is visible in
the Fiedler value---but none orders interventions reliably within a portfolio.
Placement methods built on grid-strength or Kron-reduced-Laplacian surrogates
treat converters as homogeneous and, for grid-forming conversions, can prove that
each conversion helps~\cite{yang2021gfm,xin2025howmany,huang2022gridstructure};
the reversals reported here arise in exactly the setting those surrogates
abstract away, heterogeneous machines and converters with their own controls.
Offline priority lists of switching actions~\cite{khaji2017switching} are fixed
rankings of the kind examined in Section~\ref{sec:results}-C.

\emph{Relation to eigenvalue sensitivity.} Equation~\eqref{eq:tot} is
classical, and a recent preprint~\cite{joswigjones2026sens} chains impedance-based
sensitivities through the power-flow Jacobian to line admittances. What we add
is the evaluation of the portfolio-conditioned total derivative as a
\emph{ranking}, on held-out policies and parameter draws, against finite
re-solved reinforcements at three magnitudes and against static indices chosen
in advance and after the fact; and the decomposition that locates the
re-equilibration term in network and operating-point coordinates.

\emph{Planning interpretation.} Two practical consequences follow. First, a
replacement sequence evaluated with a fixed weak-bus list will choose a
materially worse next unit in about a third of stable contexts on this
benchmark, mostly because the list cannot anticipate which replacements push the
system onto fast converter-control modes. Second, the reinforcement ranking
should be recomputed for the portfolio in which the reinforcement is applied;
its cost is one factorization of the equilibrium Jacobian per portfolio and
one back-substitution per candidate branch, instead of a re-solved equilibrium
and eigenanalysis per candidate.

\emph{What the Laplacian does not capture.} Earlier analyses on the same
benchmark found that the graph modes carrying the critical mode's energy are not
the lowest Laplacian modes (Jaccard overlap 0.2 with the three lowest) and that
witness changes rarely coincide with modal-support changes (3 of 9 events). With
the mixing result above, the evidence points to the same conclusion: the
contextual structure lives in the controlled operator, not in the static spectrum
of the network.

\emph{Literature scope.} The gap statements rest on a focused search of
2020--2026 journal and conference records (57 verified entries, arXiv only where
no peer-reviewed version exists) without full-text database access; they are
stated as the absence of such results within that search.

\emph{Limitations.} All results are phasor-domain small-signal results on one
benchmark with one custom GFL model; the cross-model holdout shows that
reversals and branch rankings are converter-model-specific, and no grid-forming
model was tested. Converter limits are not modeled, and the fast control-mode
instabilities that drive the fixed-ranking failure are those of an idealized
converter. Reversal magnitudes are a few hundredths of s$^{-1}$. Policies and
envelopes are designed points; no probability statement is made. The
preregistered equilibrium-residual eligibility threshold was stricter than the
solver tolerance, which reduced the primary GOLD-B set to \nElig{} conditions;
the full set gives the same conclusion. No electromagnetic-transient validation
was performed.

\section{Conclusion}
On the IEEE 39-bus benchmark, the marginal small-signal effect of replacing a
synchronous generator by a grid-following converter depends on the portfolio in
which the replacement occurs. Nested reversals on the same electromechanical
mode recur on a preregistered holdout and certify discrete curvature of both
signs. An oracle fixed ranking of units selects a materially worse next
replacement in about a third of stable contexts, a failure driven mainly by
fast converter-control modes. The portfolio-conditioned re-equilibrated branch
sensitivity ranks finite reinforcements with median Spearman near 0.99 where
static indices reach about 0.5. With a frozen library GFL chain the ranking
method transfers, while the reversals and the specific ranking do not. Weakness
indices should be read as properties of a network; intervention rankings should
be computed for the portfolio in which they are used.
