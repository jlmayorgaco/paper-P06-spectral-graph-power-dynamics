Abstract
In inverter-dominated grids the network damping is non-proportional: each grid-following unit contributes
phase-locked-loop (PLL) damping unrelated to inertia, so the inertia-normalized stiffness ˜L and damping ˜D
operators do not share an eigenbasis. Classical low-inertia formulas and frequency-only graph metrics then
misidentify the least-damped mode and can recommend reinforcements that improve modal frequency yet
reduce the damping margin. This paper develops a spectral-perturbation framework for the damping margin
of such grids and makes the following points. (i) The damping margin admits a closed-form second-order
decomposition ζmin ≈ ζ (0)

correction (the non-proportional modal coupling), giving a quantitative network-vs-control separation. (ii)
This correction is exactly a rational graph filter of the operator pair ( ˜L, ˜D) evaluated at the critical pole;
we show why polynomial graph filters of ˜L alone are structurally insufficient for IBR damping, while the
rational pair-filter is not. (iii) The framework yields sensitivity-based definitions of weak nodes and weak
links and a diagnostic for damping-aware “Braess-like” topology reversals, where a frequency-strengthening
reinforcement lowers the margin. The estimator is paired with a coupling/degeneracy trigger and a reduced
quadratic-eigenvalue-problem (QEP) fallback. Results are verified on random ensembles and a reduced 7-
state PLL model; full IEEE 39-bus validation in ANDES is identified as the decisive next stage. We claim a
power-system formulation and verified result, not new linear algebra.
Index terms: inverter-based resources, damping margin, non-proportional damping, graph signal processing, rational
graph filter, quadratic eigenvalue problem, weak nodes, Braess paradox, spectral perturbation.
Reproducibility and honesty. Every numerical claim is code-generated and reproduces on re-run; the second-
order correction and the 7-state PLL result were independently reproduced by a second implementation. Classical
components (the proportional damping-ratio formula, the dispersion relation, Rayleigh and QEP eigen-sensitivities)
are cited as known. The contributions are their power-system synthesis: the network-vs-control decomposition, the
rational-graph-filter form, and the sensitivity-based weak-element/Braess diagnostics. No benchmark (ANDES) claim
is made yet. We explicitly do not claim that the second-order correction always outperforms a large reduced QEP.
1 Introduction
Replacing synchronous generators (SGs) by inverter-based resources (IBRs) lowers inertia and, critically, makes
network damping non-proportional. A synchronous machine contributes inertia and damping through electrome-
chanical dynamics; a grid-following (GFL) inverter contributes damping through a PLL and current controller,
a grid-forming (GFM) inverter through droop or virtual-synchronous-machine behavior. These are physically
distinct and appear in the linearized model as heterogeneous, non-commuting dynamic coefficients, [ ˜L, ˜D] ̸= 0.
Two intuitions then fail. First, the textbook low-inertia damping-ratio formula, derived for proportional
damping, loses accuracy. Second, frequency-based graph reasoning (the Fiedler value λ2, modal-frequency sensi-
tivities ∂νk/∂w) can mislead, because the damping ratio depends on both modal decay and modal frequency. A
reinforcement that raises oscillation frequency can lower the damping ratio.
This paper asks: how much of the damping margin is set by the network, and how much by the inverter
control, and can we compute that split cheaply and reliably? The answer is a second-order spectral-perturbation
decomposition. The network term is the inertial-Laplacian modal estimate; the control term is a closed-form
correction capturing the non-proportional coupling. We show this correction is a rational graph filter of ( ˜L, ˜D),
which clarifies a long-standing negative result — polynomial graph filters of ˜L cannot represent IBR damping —
and we derive sensitivity-based weak-node, weak-link, and Braess-like diagnostics from the same object.

Algorithm. (1) Compute ( νk, qk) of ˜L and Γ = Q⊤ ˜DQ. (2) Diagonal screen ζ (0)

Honest failure modes. The second-order correction improves the diagonal screen in ∼76–95% of cases but
worsens it in a small fraction (18 /399 on the PLL model, worst relative error ∼406%); it does not reliably beat a
large reduced QEP ( r = 20). The trigger and fallback are therefore mandatory: the contribution is the calibrated
combination, not any single layer claimed universal.
7 Validation Plan and Scope
The decisive test is the IEEE 39-bus New England system in ANDES with GENROU machines and WECC
renewable models (REGCA1/REGCP1 for GFL with PLL, REGF1/REGF2 for GFM), documented PLL band-
widths, penetration sweep ρ. Experiments: (A) decomposition accuracy vs. the ANDES eigen-analysis across ρ;
(B) network-vs-control split vs. penetration; (C) weak-node/link ranking by regret against full eigen-sensitivities;
(D) Braess-like reversal detection on real reinforcements; (E) trigger precision/recall. Until this is done, all results
are stated for the reduced model.
Prior art and boundary. The proportional formula and dispersion relation (Pirani et al. 2017; Paganini–
Mallada 2017; Guo–Zhao–Low 2018; a 2026 frequency-shaping study), modal perturbation for non-proportional
damping (Pan et al. 2020), GFM placement by damping indices (Liyanage et al. 2025), and QEP damping-
ratio sensitivity (Setareh–Ghazizadeh 2025) are known. The QEP perturbation technique is standard (Tisseur–
Meerbergen 2001). Our differentiated claim is the network-vs-control decomposition, its rational-graph-filter
form for IBR damping, and the sensitivity-based weak-element/Braess diagnostics — verified on reduced models,
pending ANDES.
8 Conclusion
We presented a second-order spectral-perturbation framework that decomposes the damping margin of an inverter-
dominated grid into a network term and an inverter-control correction, ζmin ≈ ζ (0)
net + ∆ζ (2)
ctrl. The correction is a
rational graph filter of the operator pair (˜L, ˜D), which explains why polynomial graph filters of the Laplacian alone
cannot capture IBR damping. The same object yields sensitivity-based weak-node and weak-link rankings and a
diagnostic for damping-aware Braess-like topology reversals. The estimator is paired with a coupling/degeneracy
trigger and a reduced-QEP fallback, with failure modes reported honestly. Results are verified on random ensem-
bles and a reduced 7-state PLL model and independently reproduced; IEEE 39-bus validation in ANDES with
vendor-grade IBR models is the decisive next stage and the focus of ongoing work.
